## Key findings
- (p. 1) Without interconnection, each area must build overcapacity for dry years. Interconnection since the 1960s (starting with Sweden) has lowered costs and improved security of supply.
- (p. 1) Norwegian production and prices are closely linked to precipitation and reservoir levels, which makes Norway vulnerable in dry periods; interconnectors allow imports.
- (p. 1–2) Flexible hydro can ramp down and import cheap surplus power (wind, sun, low demand), then ramp up and export at peak demand; interconnectors "help us optimize the operation and use of water at home" (p. 1).
- (p. 2) Interconnectors make Norway "more affected by both high and low electricity prices" in neighbouring countries.
