Optimization of pumped hydro storage management with renewable
generation under wind uncertainty using chance-constrained programming

Natalia Naval * , Jose M. Yusta

University of Zaragoza, Department of Electrical Engineering, C/ Maria de Luna 3, Zaragoza 50018, Spain

ARTICLE INFO          ABSTRACT

Keywords:             The objective of this article is to develop an hourly mathematical model for the optimal techno-economic
Chance-constrained    management of photovoltaic, wind and reversible pumped hydro storage plants in order to maximize the
Pumped hydro storage  monthly operating profit over a whole year. The model incorporates the exchange of energy with the electrical
Renewable energy      grid to always meet the energy demand. In addition, the model includes the management of wind uncertainty
Management            through the chance-constrained technique, ensuring the coverage of the demand with renewable production in a
                      certain percentage. The results obtained from the application of the model to a project under development in
                      Spain show that the higher the risk of non-compliance with the chance-constrained technique constraints, the
                      greater the flexibility of the system to optimally manage all available resources at minimum cost. In this way,
                      although the system increases the imported energy up to 18 %, the costs are reduced up to 40 %, since the system
                      leads to optimize the available resources, maximizing the profit. The system takes advantage of more competitive
                      market prices compared to production prices at certain times. Therefore, maximizing the profit is prioritized over
                      the coverage of demand with renewable resources.

                                                                               The chance-constrained method is a technique for handling uncer
                                                                           tainty in optimization problems. In this approach, some constraints are
                                                                           allowed to be unfulfilled under certain circumstances, but with a
                                                                           controlled and limited probability. This allows managing uncertainty in
                                                                           real systems, where it is difficult to guarantee that the constraints are
                                                                           always met due to stochastic variations in the data, as in the case of
                                                                           renewable energy [47].

                                                                               This technique has several advantages such as its flexibility, since it
                                                                           establishes a desired probability that a constraint will be satisfied,
                                                                           leading to adjust the confidence level according to the specific needs of
                                                                           the problem. In addition, the complexity of the model can be reduced in
                                                                           terms of number of variables and constraints with respect to scenario
                                                                           generation which requires a large number of scenarios to adequately
                                                                           manage uncertainty and, therefore, this increases the computational
                                                                           complexity, [29], [48]. In addition, it highlights that once the model has
                                                                           been formulated, with the chance constrained method it can be treated
                                                                           as a deterministic problem with tight constraints, which allows simpli
                                                                           fying the optimization process and obtaining a solution by means of
                                                                           standard mathematical programming techniques, as used in works [49],
                                                                           [44].

                                                                               The authors of [44] compare both methods for solving the daily
                                                                           operation optimization problem of a PHS-Wind system and greater
                                                                           benefits are obtained with the chance-constrained method since here it
                                                                           only requires that Wind-PHS meets the power deviation limit with a
                                                                           given probability, which has a larger feasible region, to obtain a better
                                                                           result. Similarly, the authors of [48] conclude that the
                                                                           chance-constrained method improves computational efficiency and
                                                                           scalability significantly, in addition to obtaining better utilization of
                                                                           renewable energy and a more reliable constraint on operational risks.
