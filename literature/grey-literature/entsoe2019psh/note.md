# ENTSO-E expert group on pumped-storage grid requirements (ENTSO-E EG PSH, 2019)

**Source file:** `source.pdf` / `source.txt` (copied from the inbox; original URL on eepublicdownloads.entsoe.eu, see KILDEINFO). Seven slides; page = slide number. This is the **presentation** to the GC ESC on 5 June 2019, **not** the expert group's final written report (which the slides announce, slide 4).

## Research question / purpose
Report back to the stakeholder committee on how the EU connection network code for generators (NC RfG, Regulation 2016/631) should apply to pumped-storage hydro (PSH) modules, by technology and operating mode.

## Setting
- **System:** European grid-connection requirements for PSH (all synchronous areas).
- **Markets:** none (technical connection requirements; relevant to reserve provision capability).
- **Horizon and resolution:** n/a.

## Method
- **Model type:** n/a. Expert assessment against a requirements table (slide 3).
- Technologies assessed (slide 3): fixed-speed pump-turbine; single-shaft ternary; variable-speed pump-turbine with doubly-fed induction machine (DFIM); variable-speed with full converter.
- Operating modes: generation, pump, synchronous compensation (slide 3).
- Requirement groups: frequency, voltage, system restoration/robustness, instrumentation and protection (slide 3).

## Data / case
n/a.

## Key findings
- (slide 5) Fixed-speed and single-shaft ternary PSH should be treated as **synchronous power-generating modules**; variable-speed (DFIM or full converter) as **power park modules** in principle (with an FRT caveat).
- (slide 5) **PSH can largely meet RfG requirements in turbine mode, irrespective of technology.**
- (slide 5) **Larger restrictions in pump mode if active power cannot be regulated:** fixed-speed pumps cannot contribute to most frequency-stability requirements; ternary machines can partly contribute, but not if only the pumps run; **variable-speed pumps can contribute, typically only within ~70–100 % of active-power capacity**.
- (slide 5) In synchronous-compensation mode all technologies largely meet voltage-stability requirements.
- (slide 6) RfG Art. 6(2) requires PSH to meet all relevant requirements in both generating and pumping mode; the expert group finds this "not feasible in its generality" and recommends stating per mode which requirements apply, emphasising pump-mode limitations.
- (slide 7) Open questions: treat pump mode as demand (exclude from RfG)? merge PSH into the general storage discussion? Hydraulic specificities are site-specific and may be handled by derogation.

## Strengths
- Authoritative (ENTSO-E) and concise statement of the **technology-dependent ability of PSH to regulate power in pumping mode**.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- Slides only; no numbers beyond the 70–100 % range, no supporting analysis. The final report would be the better citation if obtainable.
- About grid-connection compliance (RfG), not about market prequalification for FCR/aFRR/mFRR; the link to reserve markets is an inference.
