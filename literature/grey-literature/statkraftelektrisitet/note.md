# More capacity as important as more production (Statkraft, undated)

**Source file:** `source.pdf` / `source.txt` (6-page snapshot made 28.10.2026; pp. 1–2 are site navigation). URL: https://www.statkraft.no/presserom/explained/elektrisitet-okt-effekt-kan-bli-viktigere-enn-bare-okt-produksjon-i-fremtidens-vannkraftverk/. In Norwegian. The URL slug says "kan bli viktigere enn" while the headline says "blir vel så viktig som".

## Research question / purpose
Explains why Norwegian hydropower plants are being upgraded for capacity (effekt) rather than energy, and how.

## Setting
Norwegian reservoir hydropower; qualitative, with examples.

## Method
n/a (interview-based explainer).

## Data / case
Nedre Røssåga (Statkraft, Nordland).

## Key findings
- (p. 3) **Total installed capacity in Norwegian hydropower was 33,403 MW in 2022** (attributed to Store norske leksikon). Installed capacity is set by turbines/generators, flow and head.
- (p. 3–4) The plant's role shifts from steady production to being a reserve "store" when wind and sun are absent. Capacity upgrading is about varying output quickly over short periods, with more frequent starts and stops.
- (p. 4) Options: remove obstacles and bottlenecks in old tunnels (head loss), drill new larger tunnels for bigger turbines (costly), or **pump from a lower to a higher reservoir**. Pumping is energy-negative but can pay off economically because prices vary, for example by using surplus wind.
- (p. 4) **Nedre Røssåga** was rebuilt from six small turbines to three small and one large, raising **installed capacity from 260 to 358 MW**. One small and one large turbine: the small one serves minimum flow, the large one peak capacity.
- (p. 4) Haugli: in a system with a lot of VRES, "the value of increased capacity will probably be more important than an upgrade for increased production".
- (p. 5) Regulated hydro is the fastest available resource, but starting or stopping still takes **several minutes**. Low-load operation is being tested to reach response in "maybe down to seconds". A related-item teaser says most turbines must run at **at least 60 % of full capacity** or risk damage.

## Strengths
An industry voice from Norway's largest producer, matching our thesis's capacity-versus-energy framing, with one concrete upgrade number.

## Weaknesses / limitations
Undated, qualitative, promotional; the 60 % minimum-load figure appears only in a teaser for another article.
