Integrated day-ahead and intraday self-schedule bidding for energy
storage systems using approximate dynamic programming

Article history:                   Most modern energy markets trade electricity in advance for technical reasons. Thus, market participants
Received 1 July 2021               must commit to delivering or consuming a certain amount of energy before the actual delivery. In Ger-
Accepted 6 November 2021           many, two markets with daily auctions coexist. In the day-ahead auction market, the energy is traded
Available online 11 November 2021  in 60-minute time slots, which are further partitioned into 15-minute time slots for the intraday auc-
                                   tion market. Because of the slow ramp-ups of nuclear and fossil power plants, these price-makers trade
Keywords:                          mostly in the day-ahead market. Only the residual energy is traded in the intraday market, where the
OR in energy                       market prices fluctuate substantially more. These fluctuations as well as the expected price difference
Trading for energy storage         between these markets can be exploited by fast ramping energy storage systems. We address the deci-
Multiple sequential markets        sion problem of an owner of an energy storage who trades on both markets, taking ramping times into
Electricity price forecast         account. Because the state variable of our dynamic programming formulation includes all features of our
Backwards approximate dynamic      high-dimensional electricity price forecast, this problem cannot be solved to optimality. Instead, we use
programming                        approximate dynamic programming. In a numerical study based on real-world data, we benchmark the
                                   algorithm against an adapted state-of-the-art approach from literature and an expectation model with a
                                   receding horizon. Furthermore, we investigate the influence of the price forecast on expected profit and
                                   demonstrate that it is essential for the dynamic program to capture the high dimensionality of the price
                                   forecast to compete with the expectation model, which does not suffer from the curses of dimensionality.
