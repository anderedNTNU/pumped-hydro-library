# Coder 2 notes on CODEBOOK ambiguities (Bore 2026)
- CLAIM vs GROUP: unclear when an opening claim is backed by 3+ studies that all do the same. I used GROUP when the paragraph describes what the studies share or do (P13, P23, P28, P29, P32, P33), CLAIM when the studies back a field statement (P06, P07, P08).
- STUDY threshold ">=3 sentences on one work": P15, P16 have two sentences on one work; I coded P15 STUDY (single work, opener and point) and P16 CLAIM (last-sentence caveat). Rule is not decisive.
- COMPARE vs B4: a paragraph can have b4 yes without role COMPARE (P08, P25, P28). Not stated whether B4 should then be reserved for COMPARE-like paragraphs.
- B1 "transition": a claim sentence that refers back to earlier sections (P29 "Section 3.5 underscored", P07 "Building on this") is a hybrid; I coded P29 transition, P07 claim.
- B3 vs GAP: "remains limited/unexplored" excluded by rule 4, but paragraphs that mix a named shortcoming and a group gap (P33) force a choice; I chose named.
- B5 "consequence": vague boundary between a finding's implication and restating it (P15, P30, P19); I counted explicit "important/must/should" statements about modelling as yes.
- B6 implicit vs none: references to "the specialization project" or "Section 3.x" (P15, P17) are borderline; I coded implicit only when relevance to modelling choices is stated.
- B7 closer: no category for a closing sentence that is a pointer or announcement; I mapped these to claim (P04, P05, P18, P27).
- B8: relative magnitudes ("one order of magnitude lower") are not clearly a number with unit; coded no.
- C1 for truncated/garbled paragraphs (P05, P30, P31) followed header pos; text damage made some codes uncertain.
