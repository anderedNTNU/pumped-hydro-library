## Data / case
**"What is long-duration energy storage?"**
- No single agreed definition. In 2024, California, Massachusetts and New York became the first states with LDES procurement targets.
- **California:** LDES is "any technology that can store energy for **12 hours or longer**". California also has a 1 GW multi-day procurement target.
- **New York:** **8 hours or longer** in the energy storage roadmap; **10 hours or longer** in LDES funding announcements.
- **Massachusetts:** mid-duration 4–10 h, long-duration 10–24 h, multi-day > 24 h.
- **US DOE:** **inter-day (10–36 h)**, multi-day/multi-week (36–160 h), seasonal shifting (> 160 h).
- Broad agreement that LDES starts where Li-ion economics drop off, at 8–12 h. Proposed buckets: short < 8 h, medium 8–24 h, long/multi-day > 24 h.
- Good LDES candidates use low-cost abundant materials, and their storage capacity is cheap to scale (bigger reservoirs). Pumped hydro is named as an example.

**"Why are we talking about LDES now?"**
- To decarbonise fully, longer durations are needed "to bridge the gaps when renewables output fluctuates". In 2024 solar and wind were 70 % of new US capacity and batteries 23 %.
- LDES advantages over gas: mostly upfront cost with minimal operating cost and no fuel-price volatility; it can absorb excess renewable output; it provides frequency regulation, voltage support and load shifting.

**"When will we actually need LDES?"**
- MDES (pumped hydro, compressed air, flow batteries) fits 8–12 h reliability shortfalls now. Multi-day storage is not critical within 10 years, and it is "still up for debate" whether multi-week or seasonal storage will ever be needed (a US view).
- Capacity markets give a 4 h battery 100 % credit, so there is no incentive for longer durations.
- Average US battery duration: < 1.5 h (2016, 257 MW) → > 3 h (2024, 12,300 MW); about 8 h by about 2035 if the trend continues. Battery prices have fallen > 80 % in a decade.
- California target: up to 2,000 MW MDES/LDES by 2037.

## Key findings
- (§ What is LDES) US definitions range from 8 to 12 h thresholds, with DOE categories up to > 160 h.
- (§ When will we need it) A US perspective: seasonal storage need is uncertain. This contrasts with the hydro-dominated Nordic system, where seasonal storage is central.
