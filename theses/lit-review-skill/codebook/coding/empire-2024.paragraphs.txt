### 3.1.2 Uncertainty  (3 paragraphs)

[P05] section 3.1.2, pos 1/3
All power system models involve uncertainty. Deriving from factors such as load demand, VRES, inflow, and prices of fuels. The growing prevalence of VRES in the power system amplifies the need to consider uncertainty in the models. Integrating uncertainty is crucial for accurately representing the power system dynamics. As noted in [Yue et al., 2018] "Without adequately addressing uncertainties, the model insights may be limited, lack robustness, and may mislead decision makers". Yet, the inclusion of uncertainty also substantially increases the complexity and thus the computational time of the models. There is often a trade-off between aspects like the representation of uncertainty and the technical detail of the model. How this is handled depends on the purpose of the model.

[P06] section 3.1.2, pos 2/3
Various approaches exist for handling uncertainty in power system models. Models can adopt deterministic or stochastic methodologies across both long-term and short-term time horizons, or utilize a combination of both. For example, the original EMPIRE model employs multi-horizon stochastic programming, making long-term investment decisions under perfect foresight while accounting for shortterm uncertainty through the inclusion of multiple scenarios for each season. The main advantage of this method is reduced computational complexity while still representing the uncertain variability of VRES. However, the main disadvantage is the long solution time and the lack of connection between seasons in a year. In contrast, most other explored power system models like GENeSYS-MOD [Löffler et al., 2017], POWER [Frew et al., 2016], PRIMES [National Technical University of Athens, 2018], LIMES [Osorio et al., 2021], Dyn-ELMOD [Gerbaulet and Lorenz, 2017], US-REGEN [Young, 2020], URBS- EU [Schaber et al., 2012] and EMMA [Hirth, 2017] are completely deterministic in both long- and short-term time horizons. In these models, the inclusion of uncertainty is sacrificed to enhance the representation of other aspects of the model like the spatial or temporal resolution and technical detail. For example, EMMA, represents every hour of the year but is fully deterministic in both the long- and short-term. Nonetheless, the developers have tried to include some of the uncertain nature of the VRES by imposing a reserve requirement and by charging VRES generators balancing costs. The LIMES model incorporates the uncertainty by choosing six days that represent the variation in VRES production and load demand throughout the year in each region in the model [Osorio et al., 2021]. Both E2M2 [Swider and Weber, 2006] and TIMES [Rosenberg et al., 2020] have the possibility to be both deterministic and stochastic, depending on the preference of the user.

[P07] section 3.1.2, pos 3/3
To recap, uncertainty in power system modeling is typically addressed in three ways: a fully stochastic model, a fully deterministic model, or a combination of both. Each method has both advantages and disadvantages, making it difficult to conclude which is superior as it depends on the motivation of the model. However, most of the studied models are deterministic, indicating that this may be the most commonly used and preferred method. This is probably because of the reduction in problem complexity, which is crucial for these big long-term models. Despite being deterministic, they all try to include uncertainty through different methods.

### 3.1.3 Hydropower Management  (5 paragraphs)

[P08] section 3.1.3, pos 1/5
In the reviewed literature there are generally two ways of modeling regulated hydropower, either as a generator or as a storage. The modeling approach chosen depends on the model's level of detail and its specific focus. Each method offers its own set of advantages and disadvantages.

[P09] section 3.1.3, pos 2/5
The Dyn-ELMOD model has implemented regulated hydro as a storage. It has a storage balance that is connected throughout the year. This approach introduces complexity, as the storage level in any given hour is influenced by preceding decisions. The approach is done in combination with a deterministic approach, simplifying the model considerably. The main benefit is the increased flexibility this storage introduces in the long term, mirroring real-world practices where hydropower producers store water during summer for heightened electricity demand in winter months. However, a major flaw of Dyn- ELMODs approach is the lack of uncertainty when it comes to inflow. The amount of inflow into the reservoirs can vary greatly from year to year in the real world and by ignoring this the model can perfectly adjust its regulated hydro production. In real life, hydro production planners have to consider the possibility of overflow or lack of water when planning production.

[P10] section 3.1.3, pos 3/5
To solve this issue regarding perfect information in hydropower management certain possibilities have been suggested. [Kotzur et al., 2018] describes a more complex method based on the superposition of inter-period and intra-periods that enables the possibility of implementing seasonal differences without facilitating connections between seasons. The idea is that an inter-period variable creates seasonal variation, whereas the intra-period variable takes care of the operational differences within that season. This method can be applied to many different power system configurations and is applicable for hydropower as it is the most widely used form of seasonal storage. The main downside to this method is the increased complexity of the operational steps of the problem.

[P11] section 3.1.3, pos 4/5
To avoid this type of complexity, an alternative approach could be to treat regulated hydropower as an unregulated generator, similar to the method used in the LIMES model. However, this greatly underestimates the flexibility potential of regulated hydropower as it cannot store energy for future utilization. As discussed in the section about uncertainty there is often a trade-off between the technical detail and other aspects of the model. In long-term power system models with extended time horizons, detailed spatial representation and high temporal resolution are prioritized over a comprehensive hydropower formulation. While the ideal model would encompass both aspects, achieving this balance may prove challenging or unfeasible.

[P12] section 3.1.3, pos 5/5
Another aspect of hydropower management in power system models is how they consider the separation between pumped hydro and regulated hydro. While in reality, these two share reservoirs, this may not always be feasible in modelling. One of the reasons that they usually are separated is because of their characteristics. Regulated hydro relies solely on uncertain and uncontrollable inflow for reservoir replenishment, whereas pumped hydro can actively pump water at any time if economically viable. Consequently, models like the LIMES model, implement regulated hydro as a generator and pumped hydro as a storage separately [Osorio et al., 2021]. However, this separation may result in countries with a predominant share of regulated hydro, such as Norway, missing out on the significant flexibility that hydropower could offer. Conversely, a few models, such as the Dyn-ELMOD model, have combined the two into a single storage [Gerbaulet and Lorenz, 2017]. This storage can be filled up by both inflows and by pumping water up from a lower reservoir. With this combined storage the model can utilize the full flexibility potential of the regulated hydro reservoirs and pumped hydro plants. Nevertheless, a drawback of this method is that the model can now fill up reservoirs through pumping that do not have PHES.

### 3.2 This Thesis Contribution to the Power System Optimization Research  (2 paragraphs)

[P13] section 3.2, pos 1/2
To the best of the authors' knowledge, there are very few or no other power system models that consider the combination of connection between periods within a year with uncertainty in the operational stage. While the E2M2 model has a connection between periods and is modeled in two ways, both stochastic and deterministic, the stochasticity is related to wind intermittency, whereas the focus of this master's thesis is hydropower. The combination of connection between seasons and uncertainty is particularly interesting to model as it may more realistically represent the flexibility that hydropower can provide in long-term power system models with high shares of renewable energy sources. As presented in Section 2.1, hydropower is crucial for enabling the integration of VRES by providing balancing and flexibility services [IRENA, 2023]. Thus, it is important to model it as realistic as possible.

[P14] section 3.2, pos 2/2
Based on the literature review, it appears that the primary challenge in implementing this concept within existing medium- and long-term power system optimization models lies in the combination of two key issues. The first one is the difficulty of managing stochasticity, and the second is capturing the flexibility potential of hydropower without making the model too complex. The combination of these two issues is difficult to model simply and realistically. It is therefore the main challenge this extension of EMPIRE faces.
