## Record format (`segmented/<id>.jsonl`)
One block per line, in reading order:
- `kind`: one of `heading`, `runin`, `prose`, `list`, `caption`, `equation`, `footnote`, `other` (table and figure text), `excluded`;
- `chapter`, `chapter_title`, `section`, `section_title`, `page`, `text`;
- `sentences`: for prose blocks only.

Display equations inside a paragraph become the token `[EQ]`, so the paragraph stays whole. Measurements must strip it.

## How segmentation works
- **Headings:**
  - numbered titles ("2.1.3 Title"), "Chapter N" lines and font size or weight compared with the thesis's own body text;
  - section numbers set on their own line are joined to their titles;
  - body-size numbered headings within the current chapter are accepted;
  - a short bold line followed by body text becomes a run-in heading.
- **Main text:**
  - from the real chapter 1, which is the first one whose chapter 2 starts at least two pages later (table-of-contents entries sit on one page);
  - chapters are followed while their numbers increase;
  - it ends at the first References or Bibliography after the last chapter, or at a numbered "Appendix" chapter;
  - appendices and reference lists *inside* papers are skipped until the next chapter;
  - a "chapter" with an unexpected number, such as a numbered hypothesis, is not a chapter.
- **Removed:**
  - running headers and footers (repeated margin lines, short lines in the top and bottom zones, "Chapter N." and section headers);
  - page numbers, including printed page numbers glued to a page's first line;
  - table-of-contents and list-of-figures pages.
- **Paragraphs:**
  - a new paragraph starts at a vertical gap of more than 1.45× the line spacing, at a first-line indent, at a list marker, or after a finished line at a page break;
  - continuations that start in lowercase are rejoined;
  - hyphenated line breaks are joined.
- **Demoted from prose to `other`:**
  - blocks under 5 words;
  - blocks under 12 words without final punctuation;
  - blocks under 30 words with no sentence at all;
  - blocks where at least 25 % of tokens are numbers;
  - single "sentences" over 70 words.
- **Repairs:**
  - broken diacritics ("Aasg˚ard" → "Aasgård");
  - the ligature loss in `bore-brensholm-2025`, which was printed to PDF on iOS ("e!ciency" → "efficiency", 'di"erent' → "different").

## Validation (phase 1 gate)
- **Chapter outlines:** checked for all 14 theses against their tables of contents. Every chapter is found, in order, with the correct titles.
- **Paragraph samples:** five independent random samples (56–70 paragraphs each, new seeds each time, judged by reading each one's start and end). The segmenter was improved between rounds.
- **Final sample (42 paragraphs):** about 2 of 42 were non-prose counted as prose, and about 3 were minor fragments of split paragraphs. After the last rule (blocks under 5 words demoted), the misclassification is estimated at about 2–5 %, within the gate (≤ about 5 %).
- **Corpus-wide checks:**
  - 4.2 % of prose paragraphs start in lowercase. Some of these are legitimate continuations after a display equation ("where *x* is …").
  - 47 of 3,067 start with a digit; most of these are legitimate ("2025 saw …", "1.77 …" table fragments).

## Totals (2026-11-08)
3,067 prose paragraphs, about 275,000 words of prose, about 13,500 sentences, and 121 chapters across 14 theses.

## Known limitations
- **`green-hydrogen-2024`, chapters 3 and 5,** are excluded (`kind: excluded`). These are two-column journal articles with a narrow gutter, where pdfminer merges characters across columns. A tighter extraction setting fragmented the lines instead. The thesis still contributes chapters 1, 2, 4 and 6 (about 5,200 words).
- Residual errors are about 2–5 % of paragraphs: small split fragments and occasional table text. Statistics are computed over thousands of paragraphs, so these errors shift no median noticeably, but single paragraphs should be read with care.
- Run-in headings set *inline* in the same line as the text ("Contracting flexibility. The volume …") stay part of the paragraph.
- In theses with numeric citations, a citation like "[18]" is kept in the text; equation-number references like "(4.5)" are kept too.
