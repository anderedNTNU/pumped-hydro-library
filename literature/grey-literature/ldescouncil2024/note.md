# LDES Council 2024 Annual Report

**Fetched:** 2026-11-04 from https://ldescouncil.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/2024LDESAnnualReport-1.pdf, `%PDF` OK, 71 pages (16 MB), `source.pdf` / `source.txt`.
Page numbers below are the report's printed page numbers, which equal the PDF page numbers. Many figures are graphics, so their extracted text is garbled.
Structure: Four Key Findings (pp. 6–28); Part 1, The LDES Imperative (p. 29); Part 2, Market Readiness (p. 37); Part 3, Project Tracker (p. 51); Part 4, Seven Enablers (p. 59); Part 5, Actions (p. 63).

## Research question / purpose
n/a. An advocacy report by an industry body: why LDES is needed, how mature the technologies are, a project pipeline, and the policy enablers it recommends.

## Setting
- **System:** global; all LDES families (electrochemical, mechanical including pumped hydro, thermal, chemical).
- **Markets:** lists revenue streams: resource adequacy capacity, energy, reserve capacity, grid stability and black start (p. 34). Day-ahead and real-time energy, frequency response and inertia (p. 18).
- **Horizon:** 2030–2040 needs.

## Method
n/a (industry data and member data, Systemiq analysis).

## Data / case
- **Definition** (p. 8, repeated on p. 46): "The term 'LDES' encompasses technologies that store energy in various forms – including electrochemical, mechanical, thermal and chemical – with a discharge duration of eight hours or more. These resources hold energy or heat for extended periods, ranging from hours to days, weeks or even seasons."
- Time shifting "across daily, monthly and even yearly variations" (p. 7).
- Four families: mechanical, including pumped hydro, has durations of "hours to days". Chemical (hydrogen) is labelled "seasonal" (Fig. 3, p. 11; Fig. 16, p. 31).
- Fig. 23 (p. 38), market readiness: PHS is "Commercial", round trip **70–85 %**. The garbled extraction also shows "0–10" and "5–12" in the duration and footprint columns, so read the figure in the PDF before using them (unverified). Closed-loop PHS and run-of-river: round trip 50–80 % (unverified extraction).
- Capacity accreditation by duration (Fig. 5, p. 15, PJM/Astrape): 4 h 57 %, 6 h 65 %, 8 h 68 %, 10 h 78 %, 100 h 94 % (column-to-value mapping read from garbled text; unverified).
- Needs: up to **8 TW of LDES by 2040**, > 1 TW by 2030 (p. 16). Calls for a global storage target of 1.5 TW by 2030 (p. 16).
- Pipeline: about 120 GW installed, 113 GW planned, 222 GW projected by 2035 (p. 21). p. 52 gives **115 GW in 2023 → about 222 GW in 2035** and calls this "about 40 %" growth, which is inconsistent with the numbers (that is about +93 %).
- **About 90 % of deployed LDES capacity today is pumped hydropower**, projected to fall to about 76 % by 2035 (p. 52, Fig. 33).
- Europe and North America have large existing PHS fleets "with many projects of 100 MW and over" (p. 53).
- Investors need long-term revenue visibility because LDES is capex-heavy with low operating cost (p. 46). Not all markets allow full wholesale participation by LDES (p. 34). Pumped hydro is location-restricted (p. 48).
- Example: Lewis Ridge PHS (Kentucky), 287 MW with 8 h of storage (p. 41).

## Key findings
- (p. 8) LDES means a discharge duration of at least 8 h, holding energy for hours to seasons.
- (p. 7) LDES provides time shifting across daily, monthly and yearly variations.
- (p. 52) About 90 % of current LDES capacity is pumped hydro.
- (p. 34) LDES can earn energy, reserve capacity, adequacy and black-start revenues.

## Strengths
- A widely cited industry definition (≥ 8 h) and current pipeline numbers. Shows PHS dominance of today's LDES.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- Advocacy document by an industry association, not neutral and not peer-reviewed. Figures are partly member-reported. There is an internal inconsistency (the "40 %" growth on p. 52). Little that is Nordic-specific.
