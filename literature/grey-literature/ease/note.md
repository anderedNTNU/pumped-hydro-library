## Data / case
**C. Key performance data** (p. 1):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Power range | 10 MW – 3.0 GW (footnote: "in general no limitation") |
| Energy range | up to some 100 GWh |
| Discharge time | min – some 10 h (wording ambiguous: "some 10h" may mean "some tens of hours") |
| Cycle life | technically unlimited |
| Life duration | > 80 years |
| Reaction time | some seconds – few minutes |
| Efficiency | 70–85 % (cycle efficiency) |
| Energy (power) density | 0.5–3 Wh/kg |
| CAPEX, energy | 40–150 €/kWh |
| CAPEX, power | 400–1,500 €/kW |

- **A. Physical principles** (p. 1): water is pumped to the upper reservoir in periods of low demand and high availability of electricity, and released on demand "within a short reaction time". PHS balances peak and off-peak periods, levels other generation and stabilises the grid.
- **B. Components** (p. 1): two reservoirs, a power waterway, and a power station with ternary sets or pump-turbines.
- **D. Design variants** (p. 1): variable speed; synchronous or asynchronous motor-generators; hydraulic short circuit; black start; **daily, weekly or seasonal storage**; river PHS; seawater PHS.
- **2. State of the art** (p. 2): the most mature storage concept by installed capacity and storage volume. Provides frequency, primary and voltage control, and can switch between modes "within seconds". Synchronous motor-generators pump efficiently at 90–100 % of nominal power; DFIM (doubly fed induction machines) can pump at 65–100 % of nominal power.
- **3. Future developments** (p. 2): hydraulic gravity storage and underground ("underfloor") PHS, both at an early stage.
- **4. Relevance in Europe** (p. 2): > 170 GW PHS worldwide; Europe 57 GW (about 33 %). IEA scenarios: 91–188 GW in 2050 (wording unclear: "PHP capacity", probably Europe). Growth 1.2–3.6 GW/yr; about €1.2–3.6 bn/yr at 1,000 €/kW.

## Key findings
- (p. 1) PHS: 10 MW–3 GW, energy up to some 100 GWh, discharge from minutes to "some 10 h", reaction seconds to minutes, 70–85 % efficiency, life > 80 years.
- (p. 1) Daily, weekly and seasonal storage are listed as design variants.
- (p. 2) Variable-speed (DFIM) units widen the pumping range to 65–100 % of P_N, against 90–100 % for synchronous machines.

## Strengths
- A compact table of the standard PHS parameters from the European storage association.
- Explicitly lists seasonal storage and pumping-range limits that matter for our pump-power modelling.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- No date, author or references for the numbers. "Some 10h" is ambiguous. The figures are dated (installed capacity, costs).
