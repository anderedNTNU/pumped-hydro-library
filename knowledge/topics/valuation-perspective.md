# Valuation perspective: system vs owner, price-maker vs price-taker

What we established on 2026-11-10 while rewriting 5.2 of the literature chapter. Every claim here was checked in the paper text (`paper.txt`), not only the notes.

## System perspective
- **What the models optimise:** one objective for the whole system. Helseth et al. 2013 (`helseth2013`): maximise expected income from sales minus generation and purchase costs, Iceland, DC power flow (p. 1427). Henden et al. 2016 (`henden2016economic`): EMPS maximises socio-economic surplus (pp. 117–118). Helseth et al. 2018 (`helseth2017`): minimise expected cost, which with price-elastic demand "is equivalent to maximising socio-economic surplus" (p. 445). Dallinger et al. 2019 (`dallinger2019socioeconomic`): minimise generation plus balancing-procurement cost, fixed demand (pp. 484–485). Not all four say "socio-economic surplus"; "optimise the system as a whole under one objective" is true for all.
- **Prices are an output** inside the modelled region (duals of the area or nodal balances: Helseth 2013 p. 1427 "nodal prices"); only the areas outside the model (DE/NL/UK in Henden; GB/DE/NL/PL/RU in Helseth 2018) enter as exogenous price series.
- **Purposes:** value expansions for the system (Henden, Helseth 2018, Dallinger); produce the price forecasts producers' models take as input (Helseth, Mo & Helseth 2023, `helseth2023hydropower`, pp. 5, 8); schedule the system where one operator controls it (Brazil, NZ, Iceland).
- **Why it is not the owner's problem** (`helseth2023hydropower` p. 7): "In the deregulated Norwegian system, the producer … does not have an explicit obligation to cover electricity demand. The overall scheduling problem can then be reformulated and distributed to the producer … Given a forecast of future market prices: maximize the expected profit". Price-taking "is believed to hold fairly well for the Nordic market".
- **Henden 2016, read off the figures:** the socio-economic optimum (950 MW PSP, 14,100 MW cable) is *also* profitable for the owners (about 40 kEUR/MW/a, Fig. 3b), so it does not show "system says build, owner gets nothing". Large PSP (6,650 MW+) gives owners about zero and lowers surplus too. The conclusion (p. 122) says consumers lose and producers gain from large PSP, the opposite of its results section (p. 120) and Figs. 1b/2a. Details in the note.
- **The one study where welfare rises while the owner's value falls:** Sioshansi et al. 2009 (`sioshansi2009estimating`, pp. 275, 277): a 1 GW device in PJM raises consumer surplus by more than producers lose, but the gain "will not necessarily be captured by a private-sector investor who relies on arbitrage", whose own value falls about 10 % (more than 20 % in earlier years). Not strategic: the device just moves prices.

## Owner perspective
- **Price-maker representations:** linear price–quantity relationship estimated "by experience or historical data" and constant over the year (`abgottspon2013stochastic`, pp. 1, 4); hourly residual demand curves with one binary per step, 23 M variables, about 30 h (`baslis2011midterm`, pp. 1858, 1861–1862); Baslis p. 1863: the price-maker withholds capacity at peak, "for a price-taker hydro producer, an opposite management of water resources would be optimal". Revenue functions become non-convex, "sawtooth shaped", so SDDP needs tailor-made extensions (`fullnerRebennack2025`, p. 460). Price-making in the aFRR market changed profit by about 0.1 % but cut spillage 29 % (`perezdiaz2020medium`, pp. 7–8). Whether a producer moves prices depends on its size relative to the market (`perezdiaz2020medium` p. 3; `perezdiaz2015trends` p. 772: reserve markets are small, so bids there move prices more).
- **Why studies assume a price-taker:** size relative to the price zone (`bore2026` p. 3; `ak2019quantifying` p. 741); market structure (`helseth2016` p. 934; `helseth2023hydropower` p. 7; `alic2023optimal` p. 24); tractability, "because of the price-taking assumption, the model is a linear program" (`sioshansi2009estimating` p. 270).
- **Not in the library:** McDermott 2020 (Norwegian market power), Baslis & Bakirtzis 2011 is filed but not in `references.bib`, and two owner studies cited in the group's old text (`S0360319915316530`, `S2352467725002711`).
