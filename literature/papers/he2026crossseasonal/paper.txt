                                        1. Introduction

                                              With the proposal of carbon-peaking and carbon-neutrality goals, China's energy
                                        system is accelerating its transition toward a new-type power system dominated by wind

                          and photovoltaic (PV) power [1]. Meanwhile, emerging energy carriers and multi-energy
                          integration pathways, including hydrogen-based energy systems, have attracted increas-
                          ing attention as complementary approaches for large-scale renewable energy integration
                          and energy transportation [2]. However, the strong volatility and randomness of renew-
                          able energy outputs increase power-balancing difficulties and impose higher flexibility
                          requirements on power systems [3,4]. Especially under high renewable penetration, the spa-
                          tiotemporal mismatch within the power system becomes more pronounced, and large-scale
                          wind and PV curtailment occurs from time to time, weakening renewable accommodation
                          capability and constraining further development [5,6]. Therefore, improving renewable
                          energy accommodation capability through coordinated development of flexible resources,
                          energy storage, and multi-energy complementary systems has become an essential pathway
                          for achieving high-level renewable energy utilization. Against this backdrop, building
                          power sources with strong regulation capability has become a key pathway for developing
                          the new-type power system. Among these, hydropower and pumped-storage hydropower
                          combine both generation and storage attributes, occupying an important strategic position
                          in smoothing wind­PV fluctuations and enhancing system flexibility [7,8]. Meanwhile,
                          cascade hydropower can effectively promote renewable accommodation through reservoir
                          regulation [9]; however, hydropower alone or conventional pumped storage alone
                          still struggles to fully cope with the impact of high-proportion wind­PV integration.
                          Hydropower output during the dry season is constrained by seasonal runoff fluctua-
                          tions, sharply reducing regulation capability; pumped storage, as a typical bidirectional
                          "electricity­water energy" coupled system, exhibits significant fluctuation in its regulation
                          capability with seasonal alternation and reservoir storage rise and fall, and its regulation
                          value varies markedly under different operating strategies [10]. For this reason, building
                          a multi-energy complementary system based on hydropower, led by wind­PV, and cen-
                          tered on pumped storage as the regulation core has become a current research hotspot.
                          Meanwhile, the development of smart grid technologies and advanced communication
                          infrastructures has provided technical support for real-time monitoring, information ex-
                          change, and coordinated operation of modern power systems, which is beneficial for
                          improving the flexibility and intelligent management capability of renewable energy inte-
                          gration systems [11,12]. Among these, a hybrid pumped-storage power station (hereinafter
                          referred to as a "hybrid station"), which combines both conventional hydropower and
                          pumped-storage functions, demonstrates greater regulation flexibility and accommodation
                          capability in cascade-reservoir regions. Studies show that by releasing long-duration regula-
                          tion capability and shaving peaks/filling valleys, hybrid storage can significantly improve
                          renewable utilization, providing effective support for grid integration of high-proportion
                          renewable energy.

                                Existing studies have investigated the operational characteristics and optimal dispatch
                          of hybrid pumped-storage stations from the perspectives of medium-to-long-term oper-
                          ation optimization, scheduling strategies, and operation rule formulation. Ai et al. [13]
                          studied the medium-to-long-term compensatory peak regulation scheduling of cascaded hy-
                          dropower stations under high renewable energy penetration, highlighting the importance
                          of flexible hydropower regulation. Li et al. [14] developed a medium-to-long-term optimal
                          dispatch model for cascade reservoirs with hybrid pumped-storage stations, demonstrat-
                          ing the contribution of pumping operation to increasing generation and firm output.
                          Tang et al. [15] established a joint optimal dispatch model considering multiple opera-
                          tional objectives, including energy production, peak shaving, bidding, and energy storage.
                          Cheng et al. [16] further proposed an operation rule modeling method for cascade hy-
                          dropower systems with hybrid pumped-storage stations, providing a foundation for co-
                          ordinated long-term operation. For short-term and multi-time-scale scheduling, existing

Processes 2026, 14, 2774                                                                                                                                              3 of 29

                          studies have mainly focused on peak shaving, renewable accommodation, and refined
                          operation strategies. Luo et al. [17] developed a short-term optimal dispatch model for
                          cascade hydro­photovoltaic complementary systems to maximize renewable energy ac-
                          commodation capability. Cao et al. [18] established a refined operating-cost model based
                          on flow optimization and analyzed the economic benefits of hybrid pumped-storage units
                          under different operating conditions. Liu et al. [19] developed a short-term peak-shaving
                          optimization model for cascade hybrid pumped-storage systems and effectively reduced
                          the residual-load peak­valley difference through linearization methods. Qian et al. [20]
                          proposed a short-term scheduling strategy for pump-station-integrated cascade hybrid
                          pumped-storage systems, improving the regulation capability of multi-stage hybrid
                          storage systems.

                                As the installed capacity of renewable energy continues to expand, hybrid
                          pumped-storage stations have attracted increasing attention due to their advantages in
                          smoothing wind­PV fluctuations, enhancing system flexibility, and improving renewable
                          energy accommodation. Existing studies have mainly focused on long-cycle complementary
                          operation, configuration optimization, short-term dispatch, and source­load coordination
                          strategies. For long-cycle operation and configuration optimization, Zhang et al. [21] es-
                          tablished a complementary generation optimal dispatch model for the Pankou-Xiaoxuan
                          hybrid station, with the objective of minimizing residual load variance, and verified the
                          improvement effect of hybrid storage on wind­PV accommodation. Peng et al. [22] further
                          compared hybrid storage and pure pumped-storage configurations from the perspectives
                          of load-tracking deviation, energy curtailment, and water utilization, demonstrating the
                          advantages of hybrid storage in system coordination capability. For short-term operation
                          and source­load matching, Feng et al. [23] developed an intraday complementary operation
                          model for a hydro­PV storage system with hybrid pumped storage, considering output
                          fluctuations of photovoltaic generation. Ji et al. [24] proposed a source­load-matching
                          operation strategy for hydro­PV hybrid storage systems by optimizing pumping and
                          generating periods, achieving improved coordination between renewable output and
                          load demand. The existing studies mainly focus on hybrid pumped-storage operation
                          optimization, short-term scheduling, renewable-storage coordination, and optimization
                          algorithms. Table 1 summarizes the research progress, main methods, contributions, and
                          limitations of existing studies on hydro­wind­PV storage complementary systems.

                                In summary, existing research has made important progress in the operating charac-
                          teristics of hybrid stations, cascade hydropower dispatch methods, and hydro­wind­PV
                          storage multi-energy complementary coordination. However, most existing studies focus
                          on short-term fluctuation smoothing or specific scheduling scenarios [25­27], while insuffi-
                          cient attention has been paid to the cross-seasonal mismatch between renewable energy
                          output and reservoir inflow. For hybrid pumped-storage stations with strong seasonal
                          regulation capability, the potential of reallocating water resources between wet and dry pe-
                          riods has not been fully explored. Therefore, developing a cross-seasonal optimal dispatch
                          model that considers the seasonal regulation characteristics of hybrid stations remains an
                          important research problem. In addition, solving cross-seasonal hydro­wind­PV storage
                          optimization problems remains challenging due to their nonlinear characteristics, multi-
                          stage coupling relationships, and large-scale decision variables. Previous studies have
                          applied various optimization algorithms, including mathematical programming methods
                          and metaheuristic approaches, to renewable energy scheduling problems [28­30]. Although
                          metaheuristic algorithms have demonstrated advantages in solving complex optimiza-
                          tion problems, they usually suffer from slow convergence and difficulties in balancing
                          exploration and exploitation when applied to large-scale nonlinear scheduling problems.
                          Nevertheless, traditional progressive optimality algorithms generally adopt fixed opti-

                                                                                                                             https://doi.org/10.3390/pr14172774
Processes 2026, 14, 2774                                                                                        4 of 29

                          mization strategies, which may limit their efficiency in solving complex multi-time-scale
                          dispatch problems. Therefore, this study develops a Variable-Phase Progressive Optimality
                          Algorithm (VPPOA) to improve convergence efficiency, optimization stability, and solu-
                          tion performance. Different from conventional progressive optimality algorithms with
                          fixed iteration strategies, the proposed VPPOA introduces a variable-phase optimization
                          mechanism that dynamically adjusts the optimization process according to the conver-
                          gence characteristics of the solution. By dividing the optimization procedure into different
                          phases with adaptive search strategies, VPPOA balances global exploration and local re-
                          finement, thereby improving convergence efficiency and solution stability for complex
                          multi-time-scale hydro­wind­PV storage scheduling problems.

                                In view of this, the main contributions of this study are summarized as follows:

                          (1) A medium-to-long-term cross-seasonal optimal dispatch model for a hydro­wind­PV
                                 storage complementary system with a hybrid pumped-storage station is established,
                                where seasonal reservoir storage rate-of-change constraints are introduced to charac-
                                 terize wet-season storage and dry-season release processes.

                          (2) A Variable-Phase Progressive Optimality Algorithm (VPPOA) is developed by in-
                                 troducing a phase-adaptive optimization mechanism, which improves convergence
                                 efficiency and solution stability for complex multi-time-scale scheduling problems.

                          (3) A comprehensive evaluation framework considering operation strategies, hydrologi-
                                 cal conditions, renewable output levels, load variations, and algorithm comparisons
                                 is constructed to verify the effectiveness of the proposed model.

                          3.2. Scenario Setup

                                To investigate the impact of the hybrid station and different dispatch modes on the
                          wind­PV hybrid storage multi-energy complementary system, the seasonal distribution
                          characteristics of the region's hydro­wind­PV resources are first analyzed; the intra-annual
                          variation characteristics of Station A's inflow and wind­PV output are shown in Figure 3.
                          As Figure 3 shows, during the dry season (January­February and December), inflow is at its
                          lowest level of the year, while wind­PV output remains relatively high; during the transition
                          season (March­April), inflow begins to recover, and wind­PV output fluctuates slightly but
                          remains generally strong overall; during the wet season (May­September), inflow increases
                          substantially and peaks around September, but the increase in wind­PV output over the
                          same period is clearly smaller than the increase in runoff; and during the transition season
                          (October­November) inflow recedes rapidly and wind­PV output declines accordingly.
                          Overall, the 2014 data show a pronounced seasonal resource complementarity pattern in
                          the study region: wet-season inflow is abundant while wind­PV output is relatively weak,
                          whereas dry-season inflow is scarce, while wind­PV output is relatively strong. During the
                          flood season, hydropower output is ample but the room for wind­PV accommodation is
                          limited, while during the dry season, wind­PV output is relatively strong but hydropower
                          regulation capability is insufficient, providing a natural resource complementarity ba-
                          sis for the hybrid station to achieve energy time shifting through cross-seasonal storage
                          and release.

                                Based on the above resource characteristics, this paper sets up three comparison
                          schemes--no hybrid storage, hybrid daily regulation, and hybrid seasonal regulation--to
                          systematically analyze the improvement effects of hybrid station configuration and
                          cross-seasonal dispatch strategy on output stability, renewable accommodation, and reser-
                          voir storage utilization. The specific setup of each scheme is as follows:

                          (1) No-hybrid-storage scheme: A conventional hydro­wind­PV complementary system
                                 consisting of the cascade hydropower stations together with the regional wind farm

                                                                     https://doi.org/10.3390/pr14172774
Processes 2026, 14, 2774                                                                                                                                             14 of 29

                                 and PV station. The hydropower stations are equipped only with conventional
                                 turbine-generator units, without pumped-storage units; the reservoirs are dispatched
                                 for generation following natural inflow within the storage-capacity bounds. This
                                 serves as the baseline scenario for quantifying the optimization space offered by the
                                 hybrid station and cross-seasonal dispatch.
                          (2) Hybrid daily regulation scheme: Building on the conventional hydro­wind­PV sys-
                                 tem, reversible units are added at Station A to form the hybrid station; at the medium-
                                 to-long-term scale, the hybrid station's seasonal regulation role is not considered, and
                                 the water pumped up within a day is released again within the same day. Therefore,
                                 the medium-to-long-term water-level process from the no-hybrid-storage scheme is
                                 taken as the water-level boundary for this scheme; within this water-level boundary,
                                 the dispatch model freely seeks the optimum only within the storage capacity and
                                 flow constraints, with the hybrid unit flexibly pumping/generating to accommodate
                                 curtailed wind­PV power and smooth system output fluctuations. This is used to
                                 quantify the regulation value of a stand-alone hybrid storage configuration at the
                                 intraday scale, providing a comparison baseline for evaluating the advantages of the
                                 cross-seasonal dispatch scheme.
                          (3) Hybrid seasonal regulation scheme: The system composition is identical to the hy-
                                 brid daily regulation scheme, the difference being that the medium-to-long-term
                                water-level boundary is obtained by introducing seasonal reservoir storage
                                 rate-of-change constraints, actively guiding the hybrid station's cross-seasonal stor-
                                 age/release behavior. During the flood season, storage capacity is required to increase
                                 continuously, driving the hybrid station to prioritize pumping and storing; during
                                 the dry season, storage capacity is required to decrease continuously, guiding the
                                 reservoir to increase release and generate using the stored water energy to make
                                 up load shortfalls; and during the transition season, storage capacity is allowed to
                                 fluctuate within a small range, balancing storage and generation needs to achieve
                                 cross-seasonal energy transfer from wet to dry periods.

                          4. Discussion

                                The results demonstrate that introducing seasonal regulation capability into hybrid
                          pumped-storage stations can significantly enhance the flexibility of wind­PV­hydro com-
                          plementary systems. Unlike conventional daily regulation strategies that mainly ad-
                          dress short-term renewable fluctuations, seasonal regulation operation enables coordi-
                          nated water storage and energy release across different seasons. Thus, renewable energy
                          surplus during wet periods can be converted into regulating resources for dry periods,
                          which explains the improvement in renewable energy accommodation and reduction in
                          residual-load fluctuations.

                                The annual cross-seasonal energy transfer of 848.8 GWh further reveals the mecha-
                          nism of the proposed strategy. By introducing seasonal reservoir storage rate-of-change
                          constraints, the model establishes a long-term storage trajectory instead of restoring the
                          storage state within each daily cycle. This effectively mitigates the mismatch between
                          renewable generation and seasonal hydrological variations. Compared with hybrid daily
                          regulation, the seasonal regulation strategy improves dry-season supply reliability from
                          93.2% to 100%, demonstrating the importance of cross-seasonal storage for power supply
                          security under unfavorable hydrological conditions.

                                Beyond improving renewable accommodation, the seasonal regulation capability
                          of hybrid pumped-storage stations also provides long-duration flexibility for future
                          renewable-dominated power systems. By coordinating wet-season storage and dry-season
                          release, the proposed strategy improves renewable utilization and enhances the operational
                          resilience of hydro­wind­PV complementary systems. Therefore, exploiting the long-term
                          storage potential of hybrid pumped-storage stations is of significant engineering value for
                          renewable energy bases with strong seasonal hydrological characteristics.

                                The multi-scenario analysis verifies the robustness of the proposed framework under
                          different hydrological conditions, renewable output levels, and load variations. Mean-
                          while, comparisons with GA, PSO, and traditional POA demonstrate the effectiveness
                          of VPPOA for complex multi-time-scale scheduling problems. However, the proposed
                          model mainly focuses on medium-to-long-term cross-seasonal allocation and does not
                          explicitly model detailed intraday pumping and generation scheduling. Although repre-
                          sentative hydrological scenarios are considered, probabilistic uncertainty of future inflow
                          and renewable generation is not explicitly modeled. Future research could integrate nested
                          long-short-term scheduling and stochastic or robust optimization methods to further im-
                          prove practical applicability.

                          5. Conclusions

                                This paper investigates the cross-seasonal optimal dispatch of a wind­PV
                          hybrid-storage multi-energy complementary system considering the seasonal regula-
                          tion characteristics of hybrid pumped-storage stations. A medium-to-long-term opti-
                          mal dispatch model with seasonal reservoir storage rate-of-change constraints is estab-
                          lished, and the Variable-Phase Progressive Optimality Algorithm (VPPOA) is developed
                          to solve the proposed model. The effectiveness of the proposed framework is vali-
                          dated through comparisons among different operation strategies, multiple scenarios, and
                          optimization algorithms.

                                The results show that the seasonal regulation strategy can effectively exploit the
                          long-term storage potential of hybrid pumped-storage stations. Compared with the
                          no-hybrid-storage scheme, the hybrid seasonal regulation strategy reduces residual-load
                          variance by 77.3%, increases the wind­PV accommodation rate from 40.3% to 96.6%,
                          and achieves 848.8 GWh of annual cross-seasonal energy transfer. Compared with the
                          hybrid daily regulation strategy, it further improves dry-season supply reliability from
                          93.2% to 100%.

                                The multi-scenario validation and algorithm comparison further demonstrate the
                          robustness and effectiveness of the proposed framework. The results highlight the im-
                          portance of considering cross-seasonal regulation capability in hybrid pumped-storage
                          operation and provide an effective approach for enhancing the flexibility and reliability of
                          future renewable-dominated power systems.
