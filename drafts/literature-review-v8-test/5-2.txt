



 

Hydropower scheduling is studied from a system perspective and from the
perspective of a single producer. System studies model several areas with
generation and demand, connected by transmission, and optimise all plants
under one objective, with the prices as a result
(Helseth et al., 2013; Henden et al., 2016; Helseth et al., 2018).
They are used to plan the operation of a whole system, as Helseth et al. (2013)
do for Iceland, or to value an expansion by the change in socio-economic
surplus, as Henden et al. (2016) and Helseth et al. (2018) do for
Nordic pumped storage.
That surplus is shared between consumers, producers and the grid, so it does
not by itself say what the owner of a plant earns, and Henden et al. (2016)
accordingly compute the owners' share separately.

The owner perspective instead takes a producer that operates its own plants.
The objective is the expected operating profit of those plants over a
planning horizon, given forecasts of future prices, inflow and other inputs
(Helseth et al., 2023).
Helseth et al. (2016) and Bore and Brensholm (2026) schedule Norwegian
reservoir plants in this way against energy and reserve prices, and
Löhndorf et al. (2013) a pumped-storage plant against day-ahead prices.
Ak et al. (2019) and Alic et al. (2023) value the addition of
pumping to an existing cascade by the change in its revenue.
For the question of what a plant's operation is worth to its owner, this is
the relevant perspective.

Within the owner perspective, studies differ in whether the producer
influences prices. Most treat it as a price-taker, for whom prices are
uncertain inputs unaffected by its own decisions
(Helseth et al., 2016; Ak et al., 2019; Bore and Brensholm, 2026).
This is justified by the small size of the plant relative to its price zone
and by the efficiency of a competitive market (Helseth et al., 2023),
and it keeps the scheduling problem linear (Sioshansi et al., 2009).
A price-maker formulation is used where the producer's bids are large
relative to the market, which is more likely in the small reserve markets
(Pérez-Díaz et al., 2015; Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020).
Abgottspon and Andersson (2013) let prices depend linearly on the quantities
a pumped-storage producer trades, and Pérez-Díaz et al. (2020) model
price-making in the reserve market only.
However, the assumed price response is itself uncertain, and it makes the
revenue function non-convex. The decomposition methods used for long horizons
then need extensions (Füllner and Rebennack, 2025).

Overall, the operation of a plant is valued from its owner's perspective as
the expected profit under given forecasts. A price-taking producer is the
common choice when the plant is small relative to its markets. The remaining
question for this review is therefore not whose value is measured, but which
uncertain inputs, markets and plant capacities the studies include.
