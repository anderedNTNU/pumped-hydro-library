SDP Recursion

· Step a) Definition of system states for each
     stage t, discretize the system states (e.g.,
     reservoir storage levels) into several segments
     to represent different operating conditions. The
     initial state is assumed to be known.

· Step b) Optimal Strategy Calculation - Last
     Stage

· Step c ) Calculation of the FCF for Stage T-1

· Step d ) Backward recursion for previous
     stages: Calculation of Operation Costs for
     Stage T-1 and FCF for Stage T-2

· Step e) Continue the backward recursion until
     the values of the Future Cost Function (FCF) in
     the first and last stages (or consecutive
     iterations) are approximately equal.

After convergence: apply the resulting policy in a
separate forward simulation.

Conclusions

 Hydropower scheduling operates within a hierarchical framework:
     Long-term  strategic resource planning
     Short-term  operational optimization and market participation

 The water value represents the marginal value of stored energy, the
   cornerstone linking economic and physical decision layers.

 Stochastic Dynamic Programming (SDP) provides a systematic way
   to derive water values through backward recursion over discretized
   reservoir states and inflow scenarios.

 Final water values provide a foundation for bidding strategies,
   system planning, and portfolio optimization in hydro-dominated
   markets
