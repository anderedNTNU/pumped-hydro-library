# Paragraph blueprints

What each kind of paragraph does, sentence by sentence, with the share of such paragraphs in the 12 reviews that have each feature (`CODING_RESULTS.md`, "Structure by role"). The blueprints keep only the function of each sentence; wording is never taken from the theses. Typical length 95 words, 4 sentences.

## CLAIM: a statement about the field, backed by studies (16 % of paragraphs)
- S1: the claim, without a study name (88 % open with a claim).
- S2–S3: the studies as evidence; one work in 40 %, three or more in 31 %.
- Last: what follows for modelling or for the decision (a consequence in 31 %), or the claim restated more precisely.
- A number in 18 %; a limitation in 7 %; a link to the thesis in 19 %.
- Use it to open a theme and to establish what the field agrees on before showing where studies differ.

## STUDY: one study in depth (21 %)
- S1: the study and what it set out to do; the author is the subject (85 % open with the study).
- S2: how: method, markets, uncertainty, horizon (the closest four report the modelling dimensions their thesis varies, seldom the case).
- S3: what it found, in words.
- Last: in 31 % a limitation; in 15 % a consequence. The rest end on the finding, which is the weaker choice.
- A number in 29 % (all theses) but only about 6 % of study mentions in the closest four.
- At most two in a row; after that a GROUP, COMPARE or SYNTH.

## GROUP: several studies that do or find the same thing (18 %)
- S1: the shared approach or finding, with the group citation (59 % open with a claim; 65 % cite three or more works).
- Mid: two to four studies, one or two sentences each, showing the range.
- Last: what the group leaves out (a limitation in 47 %, of the group in most cases) or what it establishes; a comparison inside the group in 22 %.
- This is the main carrier of breadth; it is where "most studies" is earned.

## COMPARE: two named studies set against each other (6 %)
- S1: the common ground or the dimension compared.
- Mid: study A's choice and result, then study B's, joined by a contrast word (whereas, in contrast, while).
- Last: what the difference means (a consequence in 40 %, the highest of any role) and, in 27 %, a limitation of one of them.
- Often follows a GROUP; the closest four use it more than the rest.

## CRITIQUE: a limitation as the main point (5 %; 1 % in Bore)
- S1: what the study does well or adds ("While …").
- S2: the limitation, named.
- S3: its consequence for the result or for modelling (38 %).
- Usually not a paragraph of its own: in the corpus, critique mostly lives as the last one or two sentences of STUDY, GROUP and COMPARE paragraphs (31–47 % of those carry a limitation).

## SYNTH: what the theme establishes (8 %)
- S1: a signal word (overall, taken together, hence) and the point the theme's literature agrees on (79 % open with a claim; no studies named, 0 % with three works).
- S2: the main choices or variables that recur.
- S3: where the studies differ or what they trade off; a limitation they share in 37 %.
- Last: a consequence in 32 %, often a link to the thesis (26 %).
- Ends a theme in a quarter of the closest four's sections.

## GAP and positioning (12 %)
- S1: what remains unexplored, stated hedged and specific (69 % open with a claim; "remains relatively unexplored", "to the author's best knowledge").
- S2–S3: the nearest studies, what they did and did not do (a limitation in 40 %).
- Last: why the omission matters and what this thesis does about it (89 % link to the thesis), with a pointer to the research questions and the table.
- No numbers (3 %).

## OPEN: what is reviewed and why (14 %)
- S1: what the chapter or section examines, tied to the aim.
- S2: why, or how the literature was found.
- S3: the order of what follows.
- 77 % refer to the thesis; no studies, no numbers; a pointer closer is normal here and nowhere else.

## What to report about a study (per mention, closest four)
finding 53 % · method 45 % · aim 35 % · limitation 36 % · markets 26 % · relation to another study 23 % · uncertainty 23 % · case 22 % · horizon 14 % · number 6 %. About three attributes per mention (Bore: four, with method 77 % and horizon 45 %).
**Rule:** finding plus two or three of the dimensions the theme is about, then a limitation or a relation. Aim and case only when they explain the finding.

## Sequence inside a theme
CLAIM or OPEN → GROUP or STUDY → STUDY or COMPARE (with embedded critique) → SYNTH or GAP. Break a run of single studies after two. Half the paragraphs should end on a consequence, a limitation or a link to the thesis; the other half may end on the claim or the finding.
