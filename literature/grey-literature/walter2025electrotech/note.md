# The Electrotech Revolution (Walter, Butler-Sloss & Bond, Ember, 2025)

**Source:** `source.pdf` (112 slides), https://ember-energy.org/app/uploads/2025/09/Slidedeck-The-Electrotech-Revolution-PDF.pdf (KILDEINFO, fetched 28.10.2026). Slide number = PDF page number. Group label "Ember_2025a"; alternative key `ember2025electrotech` if Zotero uses Ember as corporate author. Read: summary (p. 2), chapters 1–2 (pp. 4–33), skimmed chapters 3–6, with focus on storage and flexibility slides.

## Research question / purpose
Argues for an "electrotech" view of the energy transition: solar, wind, batteries, EVs, heat pumps and digital technology, driven by physics (efficiency), economics (learning curves) and geopolitics (energy security), will push fossil demand off its plateau (p. 2, p. 43).

## Setting
- **System:** global; regional examples (China, emerging markets, Europe).
- **Markets:** none specifically.
- **Horizon:** history since ~1900, outlook to 2030–2035.

## Method
- Compilation of third-party data (IEA, IRENA, BNEF, RMI) with Ember extrapolations along learning curves and S-curves (p. 73 footnote: solar growth 15–18 % and learning rate 20–30 %; batteries growth 19–22 %, LR 19–29 %).

## Key findings
- (p. 31) High VRE shares need storage and flexible generation among six variability solutions.
- (p. 75) Variable renewables create arbitrage opportunities for flexible technologies, which in turn enable higher VRE shares.

## Strengths
- Up-to-date compilation of global technology cost and deployment trends.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- Advocacy framing; charts with little numeric detail in the text extraction; global scope. Storage = batteries; **pumped hydro and long-duration storage are not discussed** (hydro appears only as "other clean electrons" and in a Chinese FDI chart, p. 71).
