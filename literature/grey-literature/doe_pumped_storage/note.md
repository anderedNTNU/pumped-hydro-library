## Data / case
- ¶1: PSH is "a configuration of two water reservoirs at different elevations". It generates as water moves down through a turbine (discharge) and consumes power to pump water back up (recharge). It "acts similarly to a giant battery".
- ¶2: first uses in Italy and Switzerland in the 1890s; first used in the US in 1930. Per the 2024 *Hydropower Market Report*, PSH is **88 % of all utility-scale energy storage in the US**. There are **43 PSH plants** in the US, with potential to more than double capacity.
- ¶3: PSH is "the most dominant form of energy storage on the electric grid today".
- **Open-loop vs closed-loop** (heading section):
  - "Open-loop PSH has an ongoing hydrologic connection to a natural body of water. With closed-loop PSH, reservoirs are not connected to an outside body of water."
  - Open-loop systems "connect a reservoir to a naturally flowing water feature via a tunnel". Closed-loop systems "connect two reservoirs without flowing water features".
- DOE's HydroWIRES initiative studies PSH value for grid reliability.

## Key findings
- The open-loop vs closed-loop distinction as defined by DOE (connection to natural water).
