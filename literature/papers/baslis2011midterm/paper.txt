A. Market Modeling--Residual Demand Curve

The hydro producer participates in the day-ahead (DA) en-

ergy market, by submitting hourly price-quantity supply offers

to the independent system operator (ISO) for each hour of the

following day. The DA market is organized as a series of uni-

form-price single-sided sealed-bid hourly auctions. Single node

pricing is assumed. Following the clearing of the DA market by

the ISO, the hydro producer is paid for his hourly energy offers

that have been accepted at the corresponding system marginal

price.

The hydro producer is considered to be a price-maker and,

therefore, can influence prices through his bidding strategy.

This can be modeled on an hourly basis with the corre-

sponding residual demand curve (RDC), which represents

market clearing price (MCP) as a function of the producer's

total accepted energy offer, also known as quota. The RDC

models MCP as a monotonically non-increasing function of

the producer's quota; it is also a stepwise curve, due to the

stepwise nature of the supply offers [21]. In general, the RDC

provides the interaction of the price-maker producer with all

his competitors, the demand, and the energy market legislation.

Since the price-maker hydro producer owns pumped-storage

plants, the impact of pumping on market clearing prices must

also be modeled. Therefore, a proper modification on the formu-

lation of the RDC is introduced. First, the aggregated bid curve

(ABC) is formulated by sorting the competitors' supply bids

in ascending order, for the specific hour considered. Fig. 1(a)

shows the ABC, as well as its intersection with load demand,

which is assumed to be inelastic. Pumping will actually increase

the load demand; the augmented load demand is calculated as

the sum of the load demand and the producer's total pumping

capacity. Fig. 1(b) shows the intersection of the ABC with the

augmented load demand. The highlighted part of the ABC be-

tween the actual and the augmented load demand in Fig. 1(b)
