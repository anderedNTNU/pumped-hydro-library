## Key findings (by section)
- (Intro) FFR is activated **very fast to prevent frequency falling below 49.0 Hz** at large faults; Nordic TSOs procure FFR in national processes.
- (Bakgrunn) Frequency stability depends on rotating mass in synchronous machines (mostly hydro and thermal); wind provides none. FFR need is largest at low load/production with high wind and import — mainly in summer, especially in dry years.
- (Hvordan fungerer FFR?) **Power response activated within approximately one second when frequency drops below a set level; activation frequencies 49.5–49.7 Hz**; lower activation frequency requires faster response; activation expected rarely.
- (Nordisk samarbeid) 2017 Nordic study; expected Nordic need up to **300 MW May–September** (2020 estimate); Statnett's share 39 % in 2021.
- (Nasjonal prosess) Commercial market since 2022. Procured as reserve capacity for a period; two products: seasonal profile (daytime and weekends) and flexible volume on order.
- Capacity in FFR may not be in other obligations; bids without reservations or links to other markets.
- (Markedsresultat 2026) **54.5 MW FFR Profil at uniform 120 NOK/MW/h (4 bids); 91.4 MW FFR Flex at 549 NOK/MW/h (8 bids)**; pricing: all accepted bids get the highest accepted bid price (marginal). (Text still says "for perioden 25. april – 23. oktober 2025" in one sentence — a stale year in the source.)
- (Q&A) Min bid **1 MW Profil, 5 MW Flex**; max 50 MW behind a single point of failure; same capacity may not be offered in overlapping market periods (e.g. FFR and mFRR CM); FFR Profil units may offer FCR-D outside their FFR hours; activation compensated on documented cost.
