### 3.2.1 Energy System Modeling  (9 paragraphs)

[P01] section 3.2.1, pos 1/9
Fodstad et al. (2022) summarize four key challenges that remain in the field of energy modeling. These challenges include ensuring sufficient time and space resolution, handling uncertainty, modeling Multi-Energy Systems (MES), and accounting for the dynamics of the energy transition and energy behaviors. The increasing presence of Intermittent Renewable Energy Sources (IRES) demands both high spatial and temporal resolution in models. Since IRES vary greatly by location, models must accurately represent the real distances between energy sources and the corresponding demand. Additionally, IRES fluctuate over short periods, introducing inherent uncertainty regarding when and how much energy will be produced at any given time. Modeling this uncertainty is crucial for providing results that can guide energy policy, as a robust system must account for and plan around this variability. With the transition to a more sustainable energy system, more work is needed to capture the interdependencies between different energy sectors through MES modeling. Finally, accurately predicting future energy demand and the transition dynamics of the energy system is a fundamental task.

[P02] section 3.2.1, pos 2/9
Prina et al. (2020) reviewed state-of-the-art bottom-up energy models and identified the resolution of four categories similar to those previously mentioned as the main challenge. Although many models achieve high resolution in one or more aspects, they emphasize that the ultimate challenge is to develop models that provide high resolution in all four areas simultaneously.

[P03] section 3.2.1, pos 3/9
In addition to the four categories, transparency in energy modeling is crucial to ensure that models can effectively guide policy (Prina et al., 2020; Pfenninger et al., 2014). With black-box models, the simplifying assumptions behind the model are not openly accessible, making it impossible to make informed decisions based on the results.

[P04] section 3.2.1, pos 4/9
Several studies focus on how spatial resolution is necessary to accurately model the dynamics of renewable energy sources. Prina et al. (2017) find that in their study of the Italian energy system, a single-node study overestimates the percentage of renewables by around 6.6 %. This is due to underestimating curtailments. The gap is mostly present when studying systems with a high percentage of renewable energy sources, due to the increasing importance of accurately modeling transmission networks when the potential for generating power is highly local. Simoes et al. (2017) assess how spatial resolution impacts the model of the Austrian energy system, and find that the model provides different results in the cases where the optimal capacity of wind and solar is close to the threshold capacity. In the cases where wind and solar are the clear cost-effective solution or no major regional climatic differences are present, the number of nodes does not induce large differences in the results.

### 3.2.2 Gas Situation in Bolivia  (2 paragraphs)

[P10] section 3.2.2, pos 1/2
Bolivia's energy policy has a profound impact on its electricity sector, especially concerning fossil fuels. Currently, the National Interconnected System (SIN) heavily relies on gas-powered thermoelectric plants. Bolivia plays a critical role as a gas exporter to Brazil and Argentina (IEA, 2023). However, concerns have arisen about the depletion of NG reserves, as indicated by recent reports (Azurduy and Guaculla, 2023; Hinojosa, 2023; Manzaneda, 2023). Disagreements persist regarding the timeline for Bolivia to become a net importer of gas, necessitating a transition to alternative energy sources. This situation underscores the importance of accelerating investments in renewable energies such as solar, wind, and hydroelectric power to diversify the energy matrix and ensure long-term sustainability (Azurduy and Guaculla, 2023).

[P11] section 3.2.2, pos 2/2
Subsidies for oil and gas are a significant component of Bolivia's energy policy and have substantial budgetary implications. To protect households from rising prices, Bolivia has frozen gas prices for several years (IEA, 2023). However, these subsidies, while stabilizing prices, have become increasingly costly. Sources have reported that fossil fuel subsidies totaled US$684 million in 2022 (Manzaneda, 2023). The combination of a challenging gas market in South America, limited gas reserves, and global efforts to decarbonize the energy sector all advocate for exploring alternatives to reduce dependence on fossil fuels (World Bank, 2023).

### 6.2 Literature Review  (6 paragraphs)

[P17] section 6.2, pos 1/6
Even though uncertainty in the input of energy models can largely affect the output, few models take this into account. In their review of energy models with large quantities of VRES, Ringkjøb et al. (2018) point to the challenges that arise from this such as representing short-term variability, incorporating the effects of climate change, and ensuring transparency in the modeling process. Regarding uncertainty, energy production from solar and wind suffers from high variability over short amounts of time, and small changes to the input can significantly change the outcome. Most models they review, however, base the weather data on historical data modeled deterministically, making the results highly vulnerable to potential changes in input (Ringkjøb et al., 2018). Prina et al. (2020) and Fodstad et al. (2022) point to the fact that energy models face uncertainty in a wide range of parameters, but that only a fraction uses an approach to handle it. Moreover, there is a large consensus that handling this uncertainty is currently a key priority in energy modeling.

[P18] section 6.2, pos 2/6
To handle the uncertainty in energy modeling, Yue et al. (2018) identify four approaches: Monte Carlo analysis, stochastic programming, robust optimization, and modeling to generate alternatives. Stochastic programming is an approach that can be used to provide a hedging strategy, showing the best course of action that accounts for future uncertainties. However, the approach significantly increases the computational burden of the model, and only a handful of uncertain parameters can be analyzed. The approach therefore demands that the modeler understands which parameters affect the result the most. Pfenninger et al. (2014) present stochastic modeling as a formal approach to handle aleatory uncertainty, the uncertainty that cannot be reduced through better data or a better model. They also emphasize how the approach only handles a subset of the uncertain parameters, and that there can be unexpected sources of uncertainty. This underlines the importance of transparency in models and the assumptions, as energy models are not verifiable against physical phenomena. Rather, the models are methods to examine the effects of a set of assumptions, necessitating that the assumptions are clearly stated.

[P19] section 6.2, pos 3/6
As uncertainties continue to play a critical role in energy systems, the significance of stochastic programming is poised to increase. By factoring in uncertainties, stochastic programming enables more realistic and justifiable investment decisions. A study by Ringkjøb et al. (2020) highlights that deterministic models tend to underestimate costs and overestimate the contribution of variable energy sources. This leads to an underestimation of CO2 emissions and of the necessary flexibility to manage the unpredictable variability of solar and wind energy.

[P20] section 6.2, pos 4/6
Different optimization models have been adapted to model the Bolivian energy system. However, there are no examples in the current literature of open-source models used in the Bolivian context that handle uncertainty. Among proprietary software tools, OptGen, developed by PSR, is being actively used by the Bolivian government. The model integrates Stochastic Dual Dynamic Programming (SDDP), ensuring the modeling of uncertainties such as variable weather conditions. It is used in the Bolivian context for generation and transmission expansion planning in the study by Thome et al. (2019). Nonetheless, the model lacks the open-source nature that promotes transparency and wider collaboration.

[P21] section 6.2, pos 5/6
Several open-source models are summarized in the context of Bolivian power system planning by Kyte et al. (2024). These models include Dispa-SET, OSeMOSYS, LUT, and PyPSA-Earth- BO, all of which are deterministic models addressing different resolutions of the four main challenges identified by Prina et al. (2020): time, space, techno-economic detail, and sectorcoupling. Although these models are applied in contexts with high penetration of Intermittent Renewable Energy Sources (IRES), the inclusion of uncertainty in energy planning for Bolivia remains unexplored within the open-source modeling domain. This gap highlights the need for future efforts to incorporate uncertainty into open-source energy models to enhance the robustness and reliability of energy planning in Bolivia.

[P22] section 6.2, pos 6/6
The European Model for Power system Investments with Renewable Energy (EMPIRE), developed by the Norwegian University of Science and Technology (NTNU) and SINTEF, is an open-source model based on multi-horizon stochastic programming for energy transition analyses (Backe et al., 2022). This model has been utilized in various projects focusing on European capacity expansion planning, where strategic decisions are subject to operational uncertainties caused by varying availability of Variable Renewable Energy Sources (VRES) and load profile realizations. Designed to flexibly incorporate energy system input data from any part of the world, the EMPIRE model is capable of being adapted for non-European energy systems (Backe et al., 2022). Despite its global applicability, its use has so far been limited to European contexts.
