## Data / case
**Sidebar "General performance"** (p. 1):

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Typical power | 200 to 350 MW |
| Head range | 100–1,500 m |
| Cycle efficiency | 75–85 % |
| Energy capacity | 10 GWh (a single typical value, not a range) |
| Discharge time | > 8 h |
| Response time | seconds to minutes |

| Installed capacity worldwide | 130 GW |
| Installation costs | 800–1,500 €/kW |
| Operations costs | 2 % of investment per year |
| Maturity | TRL 9 |

**Body text** (p. 1):
- Round trip 75–85 %; costs 800–1,500 €/kW (2016).
- Typical power "approximately from 20 to 500 MW", heads approximately 50–1,000 m. These ranges are wider than the sidebar's.
- Binary units (a pump-turbine on one electrical machine) vs ternary units (a separate turbine and pump on one machine). Binary units are more common because they are compact and cheaper, but they are slower and less flexible, with a reduced stable operating range.
- Variable-speed binary units: operating range comparable to ternary in generating mode (25 % to full). In pumping mode the variable-speed range is 70 % to full, so ternary is still more flexible there.
- **Ramp times:** 50 % → full production in about 15 s; standstill → full production in < 2 min; standstill → full pumping in < 5 min, "depending on the plant configuration".
- PHES can provide primary and secondary load-frequency control, black start and voltage support.
- PHES is about 99 % of on-grid electricity storage in Europe (citing EPRI 2010).

**Page 2:**
- More than 50 GW of proposed new PHES projects.
- Barriers: technological, policy, environmental. Traditional price-arbitrage operation "has proved to be no longer profitable in current electricity markets" (citing Connolly et al. 2011). PHES is now expected to provide fast, flexible response.
- Only a few European systems (FR, BE, GB, CH, DE, AT) allow PHES to provide both frequency containment and restoration reserves **in pumping mode** (ENTSO-E 2015 survey). Ancillary-service markets are not well suited to PHES flexibility.
- Environmental issues: water quality, fish mortality, fast water-level fluctuations.
- SWOT-style box: strengths are an established technology, very long lifetime, low self-discharge, high efficiency and large storage. Barriers are high investment, long payback, few suitable sites and environmental standards.

## Key findings
- (p. 1) Typical 200–350 MW, 10 GWh, > 8 h discharge, 75–85 % cycle efficiency, technical lifetime 40–80 years, response seconds to minutes.
- (p. 1) Ramp times: 15 s for 50→100 % generation; < 2 min from standstill to full generation; < 5 min from standstill to full pumping.
- (p. 2) Arbitrage-only operation was not profitable in 2016-era markets. The value lies in flexibility and ancillary services, and few systems allowed reserves from pumping mode.

## Strengths
- A compact, citable set of typical parameters from a European research alliance led by SINTEF (Harby) and Padova (Cavazzini).
- Gives ramp and response numbers that matter for reserve eligibility.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- From 2016. The cost and market statements are dated (the pumping-mode reserve rules have changed since, unverified). The sidebar and the body give different "typical" power ranges (200–350 vs 20–500 MW).
- Energy capacity is a single typical value (10 GWh), not a range.
