## Data / case
- ¶1: trade in intraday across 14 countries. Intraday "works together with the day-ahead market to help secure the necessary balance between supply and demand", trading closer to delivery.
- ¶2: interest is growing with intermittent renewables, because it is harder to be in balance after DA closes.
- ¶3: being balanced closer to delivery reduces "the need for reserves and associated costs". Intraday lets participants account for unexpected changes in consumption and for outages.
- ¶4: "continuous market, with trading taking place every day around the clock **until one hour before delivery, and in some cases right up until the delivery hour**". **"Prices are set based on a first-come, first-served principle, where best prices come first – highest buy price and lowest sell price"**, i.e. pay-as-bid, continuous matching.
- ¶6: intraday capacities come from TSOs after the DA flow results and are updated automatically with trades.
- ¶8: open 24/7, 365 days, "offering **15-minute, 30-minute, hourly and block products**". For Nordic zones, the product specification lists 1 h, quarter-hour and blocks only (`ph24_nordpool_specifications_2026` p. 6). The 30-min product applies elsewhere (unverified).

## Key findings
- Continuous intraday is pay-as-bid, first come first served. It runs until 60 min before delivery (NO) and is used to rebalance after DA.

## Strengths
- Explains the purpose of intraday (rebalancing positions, reducing reserve needs) and its pricing principle.

## Weaknesses / limitations
