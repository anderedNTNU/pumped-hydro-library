# Norway's new Energy Act: lessons to date (Moen & Johansen, 1992)

**Source:** `source.pdf` (14 PDF pages, a scanned NVE library copy; OCR text has spacing errors such as "N ORWA Y"). Original URL: https://publikasjoner.nve.no/diverse/1992/Norways.new.energy.act1992.pdf (KILDEINFO, fetched 28.10.2026). **Page numbers:** PDF p. 1 is a cover; printed page numbers = PDF page − 1 (printed "10" at the foot of PDF p. 11, "13" on PDF p. 14). Below, "p." = printed page, PDF page in brackets.

## Research question / purpose
Overview of how the Norwegian electricity market developed after the new Energy Act (Ot.prp. nr. 43 (1989–90)) came into force on **1 January 1991**, and NVE's early regulatory experience (abstract, p. 1 [PDF 2]).

## Setting
- **System:** Norway, whole electricity sector, 1991–mid-1992.
- **Markets:** physical spot "pool" (the electricity pool, later Statnett Marked/Nord Pool), bilateral contracts, a planned futures market; network tariffs.
- **Horizon:** historical/policy description.

## Method
- **Model type:** n/a (policy paper by the regulator).

## Data / case
- Transmission tariff statistics from 200 utilities: distribution tariffs 4–30 øre/kWh (avg 14 øre/kWh at 4,000 h use), regional network 2.3–8 øre/kWh (p. 7 [PDF 8]).
- Ceiling on transaction charges for remote suppliers: NOK 5,000 per customer per year (p. 5 [PDF 6]).
- Pool sales historically about 10 % of the Norwegian market, mostly spot power (p. 11 [PDF 12]).
- Four licensed brokers/traders; Norgeskraft A/S won 30+ customers with 400 GWh (pp. 11–12 [PDF 12–13]).

## Key findings
- (p. 1–2 [PDF 2–3]) Societal goals of the Act include reducing geographic price differences, more efficient use of electricity, "to take better advantage of the variations in hydro power production from year to year", and developing the cheapest hydropower projects first.
- (p. 3 [PDF 4]) The Act separates **monopoly** activities (transmission/distribution, under direct regulation) from **competitive** activities (production and sale/purchase), with "common carrier" non-discriminatory network access.
- (pp. 5–7 [PDF 6–8]) Point-of-connection (entry/exit) tariff system introduced in the transmission grid on 1 May 1992: a variable component (marginal losses for three tariff periods priced at the spot price, plus a bottleneck/capacity fee) covering 20–30 % of costs, and a fixed component.
- (p. 8 [PDF 9]) Conditions for competition: enough actors that "no single actor can unilaterally influence the price level. The actors must take the prices as given".
- (pp. 10–11 [PDF 11–12]) With an organised exchange actors face only price risk (no transmission risk except technical failure); spot purchase hedged with futures/options; a futures market is being prepared with the pool and the Ministry.
- (pp. 12–13 [PDF 13–14]) Conclusions: supply-side overcapacity, almost no new hydropower investment, large-customer contract renegotiations with 20–40 % price cuts, residential price increases stopped.

## Strengths
- First-hand, early account by the regulator; good for the historical origin of the Norwegian market-based system.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- 1992 snapshot: pre-Nord Pool (1993/1996), pre-bidding zones, pre-EU market coupling. Use only for history.
- OCR quality is poor; quote carefully against the PDF.
- Not peer reviewed.
