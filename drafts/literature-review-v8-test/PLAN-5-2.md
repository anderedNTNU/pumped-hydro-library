## Observations from two exemplar sections read in full
Norland 2025 §6.1 (multi-market participation, price-making) and Bore 2026 §4.2.3 P08 and §4.4.2 (price-taker assumption, investment perspective):
1. **Opening:** the theme opens with a classification of the field in one sentence ("Research on X often differs between A and B"; "A producer supplying a large share of volume may influence prices, whereas smaller participants are price-takers"), then the reason the standard choice is standard ("Because the DA market accounts for most traded volume, a price-taker assumption is generally adopted"), then the exceptions.
2. **Paragraphs:** 90–140 words, four to six sentences; most end on a limitation of the last study ("However, they do not consider …", "yet none assess …", "the study does not address …") or on a consequence.
3. **Numbers and uncertainty:** numbers are ranges tied to a named study and followed by a qualification ("modest gains (0.1–2 %)", "up to 30 % lower spillage, which is particularly relevant for …"); findings from one study are hedged ("might overestimate", "may expose").
4. **Citations:** the study is the subject with an active verb (review, applies, builds on this by conducting, found, argued); one group citation per paragraph to show breadth; "the former / the latter", "Unlike X, others …" to compare.
5. **Closing and link:** the section ends on what the studies leave out, which becomes the gap ("none assess how … influences long-term capacity expansion"); links to the thesis are relevance statements ("particularly relevant for reservoirs with …", "introduces an additional dimension to explore"), never a description of the model.
