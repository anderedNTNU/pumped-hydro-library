### 4.2 Stochasticity  (1 paragraphs)

[P04] section 4.2, pos 1/1
Capturing the uncertainty inherent in hydropower planning, as discussed in Section 3.1, is highly relevant in an operations research context. According to Belsnes et al. (2016), incorporating stochasticity can improve profit margins, reduce spillage, and yield more robust solutions than deterministic strategies. Furthermore, uncertainty becomes increasingly important as the planning horizon expands, which explains why it is more prominent in medium- and long-term models, whereas shortterm models often focus on accurately representing the physical system (Helseth et al., 2022). As this subsection demonstrates, uncertainty may arise from inflow estimates, price forecasts, and other climate-related variables.

[P05] section 4.2.1, pos 1/1
Stochastic inflow is one of the most important sources of uncertainty in hydropower planning, as it determines the long-term availability of hydropower production. Pereira and Pinto (1991) recognized this in their pioneering work on SDDP, where inflow uncertainty defines the operational flexibility of hydropower plants, particularly in medium- and long-term planning models. ### 4.2.2 Uncertain climate conditions  (1 paragraphs)

[P06] section 4.2.2, pos 1/1
Inflow to a given reservoir is largely determined by the weather conditions in the area where the hydropower plant is located. Consequently, weather-related variables such as wind, solar radiation, and temperature can also be treated as stochastic variables (Schäffer et al., 2019; Warland & Mo, 2016). While the former study assessed the value of flexibility under different power system mixes, the latter sought to improve long-term hydropower planning through the SOVN model. Schäffer et al. (2022) also modeled temperature implicitly through an environmental variable used to determine whether a low-inflow period had ended. This was found to have a limited impact on production and profitability, but a significant effect on water values passed on to short-term models. Temperature is also closely linked to electricity consumption patterns and therefore influences the demand curve used to determine market-clearing prices (Idsø et al., 2024). This relationship is particularly relevant in long-term models, where prices are derived through a market-clearing process, as in the EMPS model (SINTEF, 2025).

### 4.2.3 Unknown prices and available volumes  (4 paragraphs)

[P07] section 4.2.3, pos 1/4
Building on this, the literature provides a rationale for modeling prices as stochastic variables in short- to medium-term hydropower optimization models. Most studies in this field explicitly incorporate stochastic market prices under different market compositions, with the exception of large-scale system models. These models primarily focus on minimizing system costs, as seen in Pereira and Pinto (1991) through fuel costs and non-delivered energy metrics. Nevertheless, the explicit treatment of stochastic up- and down-regulation prices remains limited, representing an important research gap (Chazarra et al., 2016). In principle, uncertainty should be represented across all relevant market prices (Belsnes et al., 2016). However, treating all prices as independent stochastic variables may lead to a substantial increase in scenario dimensionality.

[P08] section 4.2.3, pos 2/4
The volume of bids submitted across the markets described in Section 2, is central to both price formation. A producer supplying a large share of market volume may influence prices, operating as a price-maker, whereas smaller participants are typically price-takers. Because the DA market accounts for most traded system volumes, a price-taker assumption is generally adopted in the literature. For other markets, however, this assumption is less clear. Faria and Fleten (2009) applied a price-taker model in the ID market, and Hjelmeland et al. (Hjelmeland et al., 2019) did the same for symmetric spinning reserves, while Pèrez-D`ıaz et al. (2020) modeled aFRR participation with a price-maker approach. Although the latter found limited revenue effects, they reported up to 30% lower spillage, which is particularly relevant for reservoirs with flood-regulation functions.

[P09] section 4.2.3, pos 3/4
Further, the volume of submitted bids affect the risk market participants face in terms not being activated in the balancing markets (Boomsma et al., 2014). Chazarra et al. (2016) highlighted reserve-usage uncertainty as an important direction for future research, and Aasgard (2020) demonstrated ways to represent this uncertainty, including threshold-based formulations. Taken together, these aspects are important both for the modeling assumptions adopted in this thesis and for a realistic representation of the market environment.

[P10] section 4.2.3, pos 4/4
Overall, the literature emphasizes the importance of incorporating stochasticity across planning horizons to improve model realism. The most relevant uncertain variables are typically prices and inflows. Price uncertainty is especially important in pricetaking models, whereas inflow uncertainty remains central regardless of how the price is determined. However, the number of stochastic variables is often limited to reduce computational complexity and avoid the curse of dimensionality.

### 4.2.4 Representing uncertainty  (4 paragraphs)

[P11] section 4.2.4, pos 1/4
In developing optimization tools for hydropower planning, it is not sufficient merely to acknowledge uncertainty. It must also be represented using realistic data that preserve key statistical properties. This is commonly addressed through statistical modeling and the analysis of different correlation structures. Further, Boomsma et al. (2014) emphasized the importance of transparency in the construction and treatment of realistic input data, as such assumptions may significantly influence model outcomes. These considerations must therefore also be carefully accounted for during the data construction process.

[P12] section 4.2.4, pos 2/4
A key statistical property in uncertainty modeling is the correlation structure across (2017) reported no market prices and between prices and inflow. In addition, the latter study found no significant correlation between market prices and inflow in the Spanish power system. In contrast, Boomsma et al. (2014) found strong correlation between spot and balancing prices, while Gjelsvik et al. (1999) showed that spot prices are correlated with accumulated inflow the Norwegian power system. These differing conclusions indicate that reported correlations depend strongly on data sources and on the methods used to construct the series. Accordingly, an observed lack of correlation should not be interpreted as evidence of no underlying empirical relationship.

[P13] section 4.2.4, pos 3/4
Uncertainty is typically represented through scenario-tree construction. Although Belsnes et al. (2016) noted that scenario-tree quality assessment is a research field in its own right, the studies reviewed in this thesis share several common steps. First, historical or model-generated time series are collected. Second, statistical fitting and clustering are applied, often using methods such as K-means (Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020; Schäffer et al., 2022). Third, discretized levels or clustered nodes are used to build Markov chains within the selected optimization framework (Helseth et al., 2016; Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020). This yields conditional probability distributions for future states that depend only on the current state, which simplifies scenariotree construction (Aasgård, 2020; Gjelsvik et al., 1999). Across the literature, both data length and tree granularity vary substantially. Studies has used approximately between 3–70 years of data (Helseth et al., 2017; Hjelmeland et al., 2019) and typically 5–20 nodes/discretizations (Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020; Schäffer et al., 2022). While the former is largely constrained by data availability, the latter directly affects computational effort. Variation in both dimensions influences how well the scenario tree captures plausible future outcomes. These findings have proved to be helpful in the development of the methodology used in this thesis.

[P14] section 4.2.4, pos 4/4
The level of detailed input data used to represent stochastic time series also varies across studies. Chazarra et al. (2016) adopted hourly prices to capture shortterm operational realism, whereas Helseth et al. (2016) and Schäffer et al. (2022) used weekly average prices in medium-term stochastic models. Although this difference is partly explained by differing planning horizons, weekly averaging can smooth intra-week variability and thereby mask opportunities for more profitable operational strategies. Schäffer et al. (2022) explicitly acknowledged this limitation and justified it by the study's objective, which was not detailed input representation. Accordingly, data granularity should be chosen in line with the purpose and scope of the analysis.

### 4.4 Multi-market participation  (2 paragraphs)

[P18] section 4.4, pos 1/2
The literature reviewed thus far has highlighted aspects that proves to be important for multi-market optimization frameworks. These are relevant not only for shortterm operational planning, but also for long-term investment decisions. Further, this subsection examines which combinations of energy and reserve markets have been considered in the literature and the extent to which they have been analyzed from an investment perspective.

[P19] section 4.4, pos 2/2
The findings of the reviewed studies must be interpreted within their respective contexts. Direct comparisons of numerical results may be misleading due to differences in market design, terminology, and regulatory frameworks across regions. Furthermore, case-specific findings cannot necessarily be generalized to aggregate system levels. Finally, the results are highly dependent on the underlying input assumptions and data used in the analyses (Aasgård, 2020).

### 4.4.1 Market compositions  (6 paragraphs)

[P20] section 4.4.1, pos 1/6
In the context of multi-market participation, the literature typically distinguishes between coordinated and sequential bidding problems. In the coordinated approach, decisions across multiple markets are optimized simultaneously, whereas the sequential approach requires producers to optimize for each market in succession, inspired by the market sequence described in Section 2.4.1 (Boomsma et al., 2014). Of these two, the coordinated bidding problem is the most commonly studied in the literature.

[P21] section 4.4.1, pos 2/6
Studying multi-market participation primarily focuses on the effects of incorporating additional markets into optimization frameworks. Hydropower producers participating in both the DA and ID markets is rarely considered and has previously been found to yield limited additional value (Faria & Fleten, 2009). In contrast, the inclusion of reserve markets has received increasing attention.

[P22] section 4.4.1, pos 3/6
Several studies have examined participation in both the DA market and aFRR markets, although terminology differs across regions (Chazarra et al., 2016; Helseth et al., 2016; Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020). Helseth et al. (2016) reported a 1.1% profit increase for a Norwegian hydropower system over a two-year period. Their study represents a sequential bidding approach and found that reserve market participation shifted production towards fewer full-load hours. In contrast, Chazarra et al. (2016) considered coordinated participation in the Spanish DA and aFRR markets and reported profit increases of nearly 25% compared to DA-only optimization. Similarly, Pérez-Díaz et al. (2020) found an added value of 4.47–4.60% over a one-year horizon, with an additional 0.12% gain when modeling the producer as a price-maker rather than a price-taker. They report that the increased value observed in Spain exceeds similar results from studies in Norway and Switzerland.

[P23] section 4.4.1, pos 4/6
Participation in the DA market has also been studied in combination with symmetric reserves. The symmetric characteristics of FCR-N makes it interpretable to symmetric reserve markets applied in the literature, although terminology varies. Helseth et al. (2017) assessed its operational value and reported an average profit increase of 6.02% over one year. Similar to Helseth et al. (2016), reserve market participation shifted production towards fewer full-load hours. Brekke (2016) further extended this market composition by including a hypothetical rotational energy market. Although no comparison with a DA-only case was performed, revenues from the balancing market accounted for less than 1% of total revenues.

[P24] section 4.4.1, pos 5/6
The sequential bidding problem is rarely studied in isolation, but several studies have compared sequential and coordinated market participation (Aasgård, 2020; Boomsma et al., 2014). Boomsma et al. (2014) investigated whether hesitating bidding strategies could be explained by differences in price levels and risk exposure between the DA market and a balancing market. They found that coordinated bidding increased annual revenues by 17.33%, compared to 14% under a sequential structure. Although coordinated bidding yielded the highest value, both approaches significantly outperformed energy-only participation. The results also suggest that withholding capacity from the DA market may be profitable if it can be sold later in balancing markets. According to the authors, this finding has important implications for market design, which should incentivize behavior that supports system operation rather than strategic capacity withholding (Boomsma et al., 2014).

[P25] section 4.4.1, pos 6/6
Aasgard (2020) conducted a similar study using mFRR as the balancing market. Although the reported added values were approximately one order of magnitude lower, the conclusions were similar. That is, coordinated bidding outperformed sequential bidding, and both approaches generated higher revenues than DA-only participation. Furthermore, Aasgard found that the value of coordination increases with uncertainty, as coordinated bidding becomes more beneficial when market participants have limited information about future balancing conditions.

### 4.4.2 Investment perspective  (1 paragraphs)

[P26] section 4.4.2, pos 1/1
Multi-market participation from an investment perspective remains relatively unexplored in the literature. Brekke (2016) found that revenues from a hypothetical rotational energy market were insufficient to justify investments in synchronous condenser mode of hydropower plants, although the analysis was conducted under lower reserve market prices. Helseth et al. (2017) argued that the operational value of reserve market participation may support future capacity upgrades or new hydropower developments. Given the long lifetime of such investments, failing to account for future market opportunities may expose investors to significant risk. Nevertheless, evaluating multi-market participation through investment analysis was not included in their study. Hence, explicitly incorporating an investment perspective into the framework introduces an additional dimension to explore.

### 4.5 Solution methods to stochastic hydropower planning  (1 paragraphs)

[P27] section 4.5, pos 1/1
The literature review is clear on the importance of including stochasticity and nonconvex behavior related to hydro power planning. To account for this, dedicated solution methods exists and have been researched for different purposes. The goal of this subsection is to explore the solution methods Deterministic Equivalent (DE), SDP and SDDP to Multi-Stage Stochastic Programming (MSSP) problems.

### 4.5.1 Solving the deterministic equivalent  (1 paragraphs)

[P28] section 4.5.1, pos 1/1
Including stochasticity in optimization frameworks often leads to the curse of dimensionality (Aasgård, 2020). One way to mitigate this challenge is to limit the planning horizon to a few days or a week, allowing the use of DE as a solution method. Aasgard (2020) and Boomsma et al. (2014) employed three-stage formulations, whereas Faria and Fleten (2009) and Chazarra et al. (2016) used two-stage models. Despite this difference, all studies apply DE to analyze multi-market participation. Furthermore, their solution methods rely, either directly or indirectly, on Mixed Integer Linear programming (MILP) formulations to represent unit commitment decisions and head dependencies. Overall, Chazarra et al. (2016) demonstrate that realistic hydropower scheduling with multi-market participation can be achieved in a DE -framework.

### 4.5.2 Stochastic Dynamic Programming (SDP)  (1 paragraphs)

[P29] section 4.5.2, pos 1/1
Section 3.5 underscored that for MTHS problems, decomposition strategies like SDP and SDDP are often necessary. These frameworks is based on estimating a future value of stored water, either by approximation (SDDP) or by Bellmans equation (SDP) (Helseth et al., 2017). The SDP solution method is often applied to smaller hydropower systems to enable accurate representation of non-convex relationships, which the SDDP framework does not easily facilitate, and can be applied to a multimarket setting (Helseth et al., 2016, 2017; Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020; Schäffer et al., 2022). Often, these non-convexities are represented using binary variables, yielding a MILP problem in the stages (Helseth et al., 2017; Schäffer et al., 2022). To handle the non-linear head dependency, Pèrez D`ıaz et al. (2020) demonstrated that the stage problems can be formulated as Mixed Integer Quatratic Programming (MIQP). Neglecting the binary constraints might lead to an overestimation of the ability of the hydropower system spinning reserves (Pérez-Díaz et al., 2020).

### 4.5.3 Stochastic Dual Dynamic Programming (SDDP)  (4 paragraphs)

[P30] section 4.5.3, pos 1/4
While SDP captures the sequentiality and intertemporality of stored water naturally, its practical application is limited by the curse of dimensionality (Pereira & Pinto, 1991). Consequently, SDDP has become the state-of-the-art approach for MTHS in hydro-dominated systems (Helseth et al., 2016; Schäffer et al., 2022). Pereira and

[P31] section 4.5.3, pos 2/4
24 Pinto (1991) demonstrated that, unlike SDP, SDDP does not require state-space discretization. Rather than evaluating the entire state space to determine the future value function, the algorithm iteratively approximates it using cutting planes. This significantly reduces the computational burden and avoids the combinatorial growth associated with increasing the number of state variables. By applying SDDP to a large-scale medium-term hydrothermal scheduling problem, Pereira and Pinto (1991) demonstrated rapid convergence and established the methodological foundation for subsequent research on stochastic hydropower scheduling.

[P32] section 4.5.3, pos 3/4
Several studies have focused on methodological extensions of the SDDP framework. To incorporate risk preferences, Shapiro et al. (2013) introduced a worst-case expectation approach and showed that risk-averse formulations are less sensitive to demand shocks than risk-neutral models. Helseth et al. (2022) and Helseth and Mo (2023) improved the linkage between aggregated and detailed hydropower representations through feasibility cuts, yielding more accurate water values at the expense (2019) proposed of increased computational effort. Similarly, Hjelmeland et al. strengthened Benders cuts and achieved improved policy quality, though with substantially higher computational costs. Integer optimality cuts have also been introduced to account for non-convexities. Abgottspon and Andersson (2013) demonstrated an example of a multi-horizon model with inter- and intrastage dynamics, solved using SDDP. It showed that their framework can represent complex mediumterm hydropower planning problems with considerable modeling detail while remaining computationally tractable. While these studies have improved the frameworks' applicability, none of them primarily focused on participation across multiple electricity markets.

[P33] section 4.5.3, pos 4/4
Other parts of the literature have focused more on incorporating multi-market participation into the SDDP framework. Using a hybrid SDP/SDDP approach to cooptimize energy and capacity sales in Norway, Helseth et al. (2015) found that the multi-market extension had only a minor impact on computational burden, although simplifying assumptions may have led to an overestimation of achievable profits. Extending this framework, Helseth et al. (2016) incorporated stochastic reserve prices and sequential market clearing. A similar solution method was chosen by Pérez-Díaz et al. (2020) in their previously mentioned case study. In addition to applying a hybrid SDP/SDDP approach to a multi-market setting, Brekke (2016) also considers the added value from a capacity upgrade perspective. While SDDP has been applied to extended capacity martet participation (CMP), the economic implications for structural investments such as capacity upgrades remain largely unexplored through such an framework.

### 4.6 Our contribution to the literature  (3 paragraphs)

[P34] section 4.6, pos 1/3
The literature review has provided the basis for understanding how stochastic hydropower planning in a multi-market setting has been studied among researchers. It underscores the importance of accounting for stochasticity, especially in planning problems with longer planning horizons. Multi-market participation has proven itself to be an increasingly relevant topic of research, as they affect how long-term water values are found which may influence the operational profit from using short-term models. As such, this might have implications to long-term investment decisions in upgrading and modernizing existing systems (Helseth et al., 2017). To avoid overestimating the value of multi-market participation, the literature review has also shown the relevance of adressing non-convexities such as head dependency.

[P35] section 4.6, pos 2/3
Helseth et al. (2016) identified a research gap related to the inclusion of balancing markets in hydropower scheduling models. Despite considerable research efforts since then, a gap remains in evaluating comprehensive market participation from an investment perspective, particularly in the context of hydropower modernization and capacity upgrades. To address this gap, this thesis investigates the research questions presented in Section 1. Specifically, it evaluates the economic value of CMP, examines how revenues are affected by different capacity price levels, and assesses how the resulting added value may support capacity upgrades of varying sizes.

[P36] section 4.6, pos 3/3
The thesis answers the research questions by contributing to the literature with a SDDP-framework allowing stochastic representation of inflow and power market prices. The methodology places emphasis on including a comprehensive market composition, consisting of the DA-, ID-, aFRR Up-, aFRR Down-, FCR-N- and FCR- D-market. Also, the methodology stresses the importance of considering the results from an investment perspective, as many hydropower producers today faces complex long-term strategic decisions. In this way, the authors fills an existing research gap of great importance for the hydropower industry.
