## Data / case
- Intro: hydro plant types are "run-of-river, reservoir, storage or pumped storage". "Unlike run-of-river or reservoir power plants, pumped storage plants enable us to store and schedule hydroelectric power generation", while also stabilising the grid.
- § Storage or pumped storage power plants: "Storage hydropower plants, also called pumped storage plants, are facilities that produce electricity by storing water in an upper reservoir…". Note that this conflates storage (reservoir) plants with pumped storage. Water is pumped "from the lower basin to the upper basin during periods of low energy demand".
- § How it works: in low-demand hours water is pumped up, with the alternator acting as a synchronous motor and the turbine as a pump. In high-demand periods water is released through the turbine. Plants generally use **reversible turbines and generators**.
- § Advantages: efficiency "**around 70%-80%**", i.e. 10 kWh of pumping gives about 7–8 kWh of generation. Plants "can come on line within minutes". PHS is "the most common form of energy storage in the grid".
- Black start: "emergency" water kept in the upper reservoir can restart the grid.
- **Types:**
  - **Pure (closed-loop):** natural inflow to the upper reservoir is **< 5 %** of the annual turbined volume.
  - **Mixed (open-loop):** natural inflow **> 5 %**. Pumping in these plants is called "voluntary pumped storage".
- Largest plants: **Fengning (China) 3.6 GW, 40 GWh**; Bath County (US) 3 GW, 24 GWh.
- Italy (Terna 2023): **22 PHS plants** (14 in the north), about **6.5 GW pumping** and **7.6 GW generating**, **53 GWh** storage. 84 % of the storage is in the largest plants (Edolo, Entracque, San Fiorano, Presenzano, Anapo).
- FAQ: both pure and mixed plants have two reservoirs at different elevations.

## Key findings
- An operational definition of closed-loop vs open-loop based on a 5 % natural-inflow share.
- Round-trip efficiency about 70–80 %; start-up within minutes.
