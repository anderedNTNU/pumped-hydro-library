# European Electricity Review 2025 (Rosslowe & Petrovich, Ember, 2025)

**Source:** `source.pdf` (47 pages), https://ember-energy.org/app/uploads/2025/01/EER_2025_22012025.pdf (KILDEINFO, fetched 28.10.2026). Cover date 23 January 2025. Printed page numbers = PDF page numbers. The bibkey follows the first named author; the group calls it "Ember_2025b" (alternative key `ember2025european` if Zotero uses Ember as corporate author).

## Research question / purpose
Annual analysis of 2024 electricity generation and demand in all **EU-27** countries and five years of the European Green Deal (p. 2). Norway is **not** covered.

## Setting
- **System:** EU-27 (Norway excluded).
- **Markets:** day-ahead wholesale prices (ENTSO-E) for price statistics (p. 46).
- **Horizon and resolution:** 2019–2024, annual/monthly/hourly data.

## Method
- Data compilation (Eurostat, ENTSO-E, national TSOs) and simple counterfactuals (avoided fossil imports via an hourly merit-order displacement rule, pp. 42–44). A solar + co-located battery capture-price model (90 MW solar, 60 MW/60 MWh battery, 87 % round-trip efficiency, rule-based cycling) is described in the methodology (p. 45).
- **Definition (p. 46):** "Within-day power price spreads" = max minus min **day-ahead** price within a day. Note: the main text (p. 26) calls these "intraday price spreads", meaning within-day DA spreads, **not** intraday-market prices.

| Wind | 17 % (477 TWh) | p. 10 |

| Fossil generation | 793 TWh (29 %), lowest in 40+ years | pp. 12, 15 |
| **Zero or negative price hours** | **4 % of hours** on average across the EU in 2024 vs 2 % in 2023 | p. 26 |
| Within-day spreads | higher than 2023 in most countries, especially summer; Q3 2024 spreads +≥10 % y/y in 18 countries; doubled and > €200/MWh in BG, GR, HU, PL, RO | p. 26 |
| Solar meeting ≥80 % of demand in at least one hour | 12 countries; > 70 days in NL and HU | p. 25 |
| EU battery capacity | 8 GW (2022) → **16 GW (2023)**; 70 % in DE and IT | p. 28 |

## Key findings
- (p. 26) Plentiful solar lowered midday prices; negative or zero prices became more frequent and widespread; within-day price spreads widened, amplified by costly evening fossil ramping.
- (p. 27) Negative prices and wider spreads "signal a business case for more clean flexibility": storage shifting solar in time, grids in space, demand shifting.
- (p. 28) Barriers for batteries: double grid charging, restrictive requirements for capacity markets and grid services.
- (p. 16) Hydro and nuclear generation fluctuate with weather and outages; hydro capacity did not grow.

## Strengths
- Recent, transparent data; clear definitions; good descriptive statistics on price shape changes in Europe.

## Weaknesses / limitations
- EU-27 only (no Norway). Advocacy-oriented NGO framing (clean-energy transition). Battery focus; no pumped hydro discussion. No volatility metrics beyond max–min spreads and share of zero/negative hours.
