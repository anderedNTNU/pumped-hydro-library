  - One finding theme per paragraph (economics → interaction with other signals → asset comparison → general flexibility benefit), each with its mechanism in one clause.
  - Repeats only the two headline numbers, so the conclusion reads as claims, not as a second results section.
- **Weaknesses:** RQs never named, so the reader must map answers; RQ3's "which incentive gives most value" is answered less clearly than the results suggest; future-work bullets lack limitation, effect and cost.
- **Signal phrases:** "The results implies that"; "The study also provides key indicators"

## 3. Whole-thesis notes
- **Red thread:** frequent explicit section back-references (e.g. "As discussed in Section 2.2") and forward pointers at the end of background blocks (to 2.4, 2.6, 2.7, ch. 6, ch. 7); fixed terms (prosumer, DFR, value stacking, the two contract names) and fixed case codes used identically from ch. 6 to ch. 8. Market rules in §2.6 reappear as data in §6.3 and as constraints in ch. 5. The RQs (§2.8) are not revisited by name in ch. 7 or 8.
- **How the RQs are answered:** implicitly, one conclusion paragraph per RQ cluster, roughly in order (cost/operation impact and years → incentive comparison → asset comparison), with the two headline percentages; no numbered answers.
- **Anything the codes do not capture:**
  - The RQs sit at the end of the background/review chapter, directly after a "closest studies" section, so gap → RQ is one step; the introduction has only a purpose paragraph.
  - Problem description and model are separate chapters, with the problem chapter structured as objective / decisions / limiting considerations — a verbal mirror of the model.
  - A single-objective deterministic study gets depth from a case grid (4 cases × 3 contrasting years) plus one supplementary extension, rather than from method complexity.
  - The peer-reviewed conference paper from the thesis is attached as an appendix; its abstract (unlike the thesis abstract) carries the headline number.
  - Sourced facts are often given as URL footnotes rather than reference-list entries.
